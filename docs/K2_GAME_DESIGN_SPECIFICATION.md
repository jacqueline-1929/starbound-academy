# Starbound Academy K–2 Game Design Specification

**Version:** 0.1 — curriculum-to-game translation  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Audience:** curriculum designers, game designers, artists, writers,
engineers, reviewers, and parents  
**Status:** Production design draft

> The lesson is not hidden behind the game. The lesson is the law the player
> learns to use.

This document translates the
[K–2 Curriculum and Pacing Guide](K2_CURRICULUM_AND_PACING_GUIDE.md) into
playable systems. The
[World, Experience, and Pedagogy Design Bible](STARBOUND_ACADEMY_DESIGN_BIBLE.md)
defines the broad creative philosophy. This specification defines what the K–2
game must actually do.

---

## 1. Why this document exists

Without a translation layer, curriculum and game production drift apart:

- curriculum becomes a quiz inserted between unrelated animations;
- game mechanics accidentally measure reaction time, reading level, or pointer
  control instead of the academic construct;
- every lesson invents new controls and feedback;
- difficulty means “more questions” or “faster timer”;
- spectacular activities produce no inspectable learning evidence; or
- standards tagging happens after development and claims more than the game
  demonstrates.

The K–2 Game Design Specification prevents that drift. Every activity must
connect:

> Academic outcome → governing relationship → player action → world feedback →
> evidence → later retrieval.

If any arrow is missing, the activity is not production-ready.

## 2. Player experience goals

### What a child should feel

- “I know what I can touch or try.”
- “Something funny or important will happen when I act.”
- “The world has rules, and I can figure them out.”
- “My mistake showed me something.”
- “I can try a different way.”
- “I can make something that belongs to me.”
- “The hard boss is dangerous, but I am allowed to knock.”
- “My character and home remember what I have done.”
- “I want to show this to someone.”
- “I wonder what is behind the next door.”

### What a parent or reviewer should be able to see

- the exact learning target and standard;
- what the player independently demonstrated;
- what support or audio was used;
- which representation and difficulty were used;
- what misconception appeared and whether it changed;
- the saved creation or explanation;
- when the idea will return;
- how the task remains accessible; and
- why the game action is valid evidence of the outcome.

## 3. Player lanes

### Robby lane

**Instructional range:** Kindergarten–Grade 1 foundations

- fewer simultaneous interface elements;
- short spoken instructions with visual demonstration;
- strong material/visible models;
- one new relationship at a time;
- more action before text;
- controlled decodable text for independent reading;
- rich natural read-aloud beyond decoding level;
- shorter missions divided by movement/offline action;
- frequent successful transformations; and
- optional advanced bosses always visible.

### Tommy lane

**Instructional range:** Grade 2 with Grades 3–5 stretch

- confirmation bosses instead of repeating secure foundations;
- longer and faster original stories;
- deeper inference, conflicting evidence, morphology, and vocabulary;
- more construction and explanation;
- reduced prompting where evidence supports it;
- multi-step investigations and system problems;
- optional advanced text and concept doors; and
- equal access to playful tools, music, Spanish, characters, and rewards.

### One shared world

The lanes are not separate “baby” and “smart” games. Robby and Tommy share:

- world events and lore;
- major characters and humor;
- seasonal arcs and family exhibitions;
- music and Spanish worlds;
- avatar status and cosmetic quality;
- shared science phenomena where practical; and
- opportunities to teach, collaborate, or compare creations without a
  leaderboard.

The system changes academic evidence, supports, text, representations, and
challenge—not dignity or production value.

## 4. Time-scale loops

### Moment-to-moment loop: 5–30 seconds

1. Notice an object, pattern, contradiction, or need.
2. Predict or choose an action.
3. Move, combine, speak, type, draw, measure, or test.
4. See and hear a direct consequence.
5. Adjust, explain, or continue.

The feedback must reveal the academic relationship. Confetti alone is not
feedback.

### Practice loop: 2–5 minutes

1. Encounter a worked or supported example.
2. Complete a related case.
3. Compare what changed and what remained invariant.
4. Solve a less-supported variation.
5. Receive an invitation to continue, create, or pause.

### Mission loop: 10–25 minutes

1. Cold open and retrieval.
2. Comic problem or phenomenon.
3. First safe experiment.
4. Visible governing model.
5. Training sequence with meaningful variation.
6. Explanation or complication.
7. Independent challenge.
8. Creation/free play or offline bridge.
9. Comedy resolution and Window Upward.

A mission may be paused at any section. K learners may complete it across two
or three short sessions.

### Unit loop: 2–8 weeks

1. Anchor mystery, conflict, or design need.
2. Several missions build one coherent relationship network.
3. Supports fade and representations connect.
4. The unit boss stays visible from the beginning.
5. A creative studio opens as knowledge becomes useful.
6. Delayed retrieval and transfer appear before the finale.
7. The learner defeats, revises, or postpones the boss.
8. Evidence and selected work enter the portfolio.

### Arc loop: 9 weeks

- eight weeks of new learning and cumulative play;
- one festival week for cross-unit retrieval, open boss attempts, repair,
  performance, portfolio curation, and family exhibition; and
- visible world change reflecting the completed arc.

### Year loop

The avatar and home base begin the year with evidence of prior adventures. Four
arcs culminate in the First Star Festival, where the child demonstrates,
performs, creates, reflects, and chooses what to pursue next. Character level
never resets.

## 5. The world map

The K–2 map is the Launch Region of **The Wonder Archipelago**, the shared K–5
experience world. It is a three-dimensional constellation of subject islands
connected to a warm moving home base. Grades 3–5 later expand the same
archipelago into the Frontier Region with larger spaces, denser systems, longer
quests, and more independent planning; they do not replace the child's home or
history.

| World | Domain | Core fantasy | Primary actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story Nebula | Reading | Sounds, word structures, evidence, and interpretation reveal paths | Hear, segment, map, blend, transform, read, sequence, infer, defend |
| Writer's Forge | Writing | Language physically constructs and revises scenes | Arrange, rehearse, write, combine, punctuate, revise, publish |
| Measure Moon | Mathematics | Relationships stabilize machines, bridges, markets, and habitats | Compare, measure, balance, compose, decompose, represent, calculate, generalize |
| Force Frontier | Science | Prediction and evidence control natural systems | Observe, predict, vary, measure, model, explain, design |
| Chronicle Archipelago | History/civics | Maps, objects, and accounts restore lost human stories | Locate, sequence, observe, compare sources, infer, claim, act |
| Plaza del Sol | Spanish | Communication builds relationships and resolves social needs | Listen, act, greet, ask, answer, choose, recombine, perform |
| Rhythm Grove | Music | Sound, rhythm, pitch, and form animate the environment | Move, hear, imitate, place, read, transform, loop, compose, perform |
| Code Foundry | Computing/making | Precise instructions awaken tools and creatures | Sequence, trigger, repeat, condition, debug, build, simulate |
| Home Constellation | Identity/portfolio | The learner's history becomes a navigable place | Dress, decorate, review, curate, replay, reflect, share privately |

World access is non-linear. Recommended routes glow gently; no academic world is
locked behind a character level. Boss doors remain visible.

At the edge of the sky, distant portals may foreshadow the later Guild Realms,
Constellation Network, and Great Conversation. These are invitations to wonder,
not advertisements or pressure to accelerate. Selected future challenges can be
attempted early under the same open-boss and evidence rules used inside K–2.

## 6. Four K–2 story arcs

### Arc I — The Broken Launch

A hungry visitor crashes because a translation-and-navigation machine has
scrambled sounds, amounts, signals, and maps. The supposed villain is partly a
punctuation robot issuing catastrophically literal instructions.

**Academic spine:** first/confirmed reading code, comparison and equality,
force/light, greeting and identity, steady beat, sequence and debugging.

**World change:** the ship becomes the player's mobile home base.

### Arc II — The Missing Market

Food, tools, and instruments vanish from a traveling market. The crisis results
from bad measurement, mistaken wants, unequal trades, and a tiny antagonist who
ordered twelve thousand onions instead of twelve.

**Academic spine:** operations/place value, narrative cause, family/food
Spanish, matter/weather, rhythm and form, needs/wants/work.

**World change:** the player opens a customizable café/workshop.

### Arc III — The Moving World

Paths, habitats, coastlines, stories, and machines are changing. Different
characters describe the same event in incompatible ways.

**Academic spine:** measurement/data, land and life systems, maps/movement,
morphology, evidence and viewpoint, expressive music.

**World change:** a living atlas records discoveries and field work.

### Arc IV — The Storm Core

A powerful device can protect the city or tear apart its systems. The rival
wants to act immediately; the sleeping scientist wakes only when somebody
mentions “catastrophic magnetic dumplings.”

**Academic spine:** electricity/magnetism or sky/weather, multi-step
mathematics, inference and argument, civic choice, composition, and integrated
design.

**World change:** the First Star Festival opens and the child's creations power
the celebration.

## 7. The interaction grammar

Young players should learn a small stable set of verbs that recombine across
subjects.

### Universal verbs

- **Pick up / place** — move a sound token, number unit, note, source, material,
  or code block.
- **Connect** — show a relationship between evidence and claim, part and whole,
  cause and effect, word and meaning, or circuit components.
- **Transform** — change one element and observe the system consequence.
- **Sort** — classify by a named or discovered attribute.
- **Compare** — align, balance, measure, overlay, or listen side by side.
- **Run / play / test** — animate a model, hear a composition, execute code, or
  conduct a simulation.
- **Explain** — speak, choose, arrange, draw, or write a reason.
- **Save / revise** — preserve a version and make an intentional change.

### Input equivalence

Where the academic construct permits, each action supports:

- pointer/touch;
- keyboard;
- switch-compatible focus and activation;
- speech or adult scribing;
- physical manipulatives with parent confirmation; and
- simplified 2D HTML when 3D/WebGL is unavailable.

Input method is never confused with academic mastery unless motor or tool use is
the actual outcome.

## 8. Subject mechanic library

### 8.1 Foundational reading

#### Sound Chambers

The child hears a word and moves one pod for each phoneme. Pods pulse with the
mouth sound but initially contain no letters. Letters arrive only after the
sound structure is stable.

**Measures:** segmentation, phoneme identity, sound-symbol mapping.  
**Does not measure:** vocabulary or passage comprehension.  
**Failure feedback:** pods stretch, collide, or leave a visible gap; the model
replays slowly without saying the answer as a judgment.

#### Word Reactor

Change one phoneme/grapheme to transform the object or event: `map → mop`,
`cap → cat`, or later `hope → hop`.

**Governing relationship:** a changed sound/spelling can change a word and its
meaning.  
**Free play:** invent valid creature names from taught code.  
**Boss:** make a requested transformation without choices or audio reveal.

#### Blend Rail

Letters or grapheme tiles ride a rail while the child sustains and blends
sounds. The rail becomes faster only through learner control, never pressure.

#### Decodable Path

Reading correctly causes the character to act according to the sentence. A
picture appears after the attempt, preventing picture guessing from masquerading
as decoding.

### 8.2 Comprehension and literature

#### Story Arena

Place scenes, causes, evidence, character beliefs, and consequences into a
spatial model. Incorrect placement plays a plausible contradiction rather than
displaying only a red mark.

#### Evidence Cores

Attach a text detail to a claim. The claim powers up only when the evidence is
relevant; stronger bosses include tempting but irrelevant true details.

#### Witness Lens

View the same event through two characters or sources. The environment changes
to show what each knows, believes, or omits.

#### Pace Engine

For Tommy's stretch reading, sentence length, punctuation, paragraph breaks,
and reveal order change the speed and tension of an animated scene.

### 8.3 Writing

#### Sentence Foundry

Movable word/phrase parts visibly construct an event. Changing the verb alters
animation; punctuation changes timing; modifiers change precision.

#### Dialogue Console

Punctuation robot misreads unpunctuated dialogue literally. The learner repairs
quotation, speaker, and sentence boundaries.

#### Revision Mirror

Original and revised scenes play side by side. The learner names what the
revision improved: clarity, detail, sequence, pace, evidence, or voice.

#### Publication Bay

Combine text, illustration, narration, and design into a saved artifact. The
system retains drafts and support history.

### 8.4 Mathematics

#### Comparison Dock

Align quantities, lengths, weights, or capacities. The child chooses an
attribute, constructs a comparison, and represents the relationship in objects,
diagrams, words, then symbols.

#### Balance Reactor

Both sides of an equality power one machine. Changes preserve or destroy the
balance, teaching equality as a relationship.

#### Unit Builder

Create, repeat, bundle, and exchange units: equal length units, ones/tens/
hundreds, or equal groups. A poor unit produces visible inconsistency.

#### Transformation Machine

The learner applies and reverses a change. Addition/subtraction, compose/
decompose, and later inverse relationships share one visual language.

#### Story Modeler

Build a diagram or equation from a situation with unknowns in varied positions.
The learner is not rewarded for keyword matching.

#### Strategy Theater

Two characters solve the same problem differently. The learner tests both,
finds an error if present, and chooses which is efficient under the conditions.

### 8.5 Science

#### Prediction Lock

Before an experiment or revealing video segment, the learner records a
prediction through object placement, drawing, speech, or choice. Predictions are
never graded as wrong; they establish a model to revise.

#### Variable Bench

Change one factor, hold relevant factors stable, run repeated trials, and place
results in a visible data representation.

#### Invisible-System Viewer

A simulation represents a field, ray, force arrow, circuit path, vibration, or
other explanatory model only after concrete observation. The model is labelled
as a representation, not a literal hidden view.

#### Model Workshop

Build an initial explanation, collect evidence, then visibly revise components.
The portfolio stores the change, not only the final answer.

#### Engineering Bay

Define a need and constraints, build/test, inspect failure, revise, and explain
tradeoffs.

### 8.6 History, geography, and civics

#### Living Map

Place routes, features, resources, settlements, or events on maps with changing
scale and point of view.

#### Artifact Table

Rotate and inspect a photograph, object, map, letter, recording, or poster.
Responses are separated into “I observe,” “I think,” and “I wonder.”

#### Timeline Causeway

Sequence events, then connect cause, consequence, continuity, and change. Time
spacing is not falsely shown as equal when it is not.

#### Council Chamber

Characters have needs, evidence, rights, responsibilities, and tradeoffs. The
learner listens, asks, proposes, and supports a choice. Civic disagreement is
not reduced to choosing the designer's preferred character.

### 8.7 Spanish

#### Social Need Scenes

The child needs language to greet, identify, ask, feed, help, direct, or repair
a misunderstanding. Comprehension appears through action before translation.

#### Human Voice Tiles

Tap or focus to hear licensed human speech. Tiles combine into practised
phrases, then supports fade for recombination.

#### Plaza Conversation

Branching but bounded interaction accepts multiple valid responses. Characters
react to meaning, not only an exact string.

#### Listening Boss

An unfamiliar approved speaker uses familiar language in a new situation. The
learner demonstrates comprehension or responds without seeing English first.

### 8.8 Music

#### Beat Path

The environment pulses and moves on a steady beat. The child walks, taps,
places, or triggers events in time without a punitive precision score.

#### Color Score

Dragging notes changes color, vertical position, label, and sound. Color is
redundant, never the sole carrier of pitch.

#### Loop Garden

Create, layer, mute, vary, save, name, and replay rhythmic/melodic loops.
Concepts such as beat, rest, phrase, form, tempo, dynamics, and pitch are the
controls of the garden.

#### Form Builder

Arrange A/B or later patterns. The environment visibly returns, contrasts, or
transforms with musical form.

#### Score Boss

Hear, read, perform, alter, and explain a short unfamiliar pattern. A creation
may be harder than the required boss but does not need to be complicated to be
valid.

### 8.9 Computing and making

#### Instruction Trail

Arrange steps to move a character or physical action. The system executes
literally, allowing funny debugging without mocking the child.

#### Event Workshop

Connect an event to a response, then introduce repetition and conditions.

#### Debug Theater

The learner predicts what flawed instructions will do, runs them, finds the
first divergence, and changes one part.

#### Mod Seed

Older/ready K–2 learners customize a safe template—dialogue, map, beat, word
set, or number challenge—without access to private records or unsafe scripting.

## 9. Difficulty transformations

Difficulty changes the relationship between challenge and support.

| Dimension | Normal | Hard | Master |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation | Goal and relevant model demonstrated | Goal clear; model partly recalled | Learner identifies useful relationship/model |
| Representation | Concrete plus visual/verbal | Fewer simultaneous representations | Translate or choose representations independently |
| Construction | Select, complete, or manipulate | Construct more of response | Construct and justify under unfamiliar conditions |
| Hints | Immediate, specific, learner-controlled | Delayed or reduced | Minimal; post-attempt analysis available |
| Language | Chunked, defined, read-aloud | Less chunking; more precise output | Complex/unfamiliar context with access supports |
| Transfer | Near transfer | Varied context or exception | Farther transfer, multiple relationships, or creation |
| Reward | 10 base points | 15 base points | 20 base points |

Difficulty must not:

- introduce an untaught prerequisite and call it depth;
- make reading speed determine science or mathematics success;
- remove accessibility;
- increase enemy speed as the primary academic challenge;
- multiply repetitive questions;
- shame a return to Normal; or
- permanently change the learner's baseline after one success or failure.

## 10. Boss design

### Boss categories

- **Skill boss:** focused independent performance of one outcome.
- **System boss:** coordinate several related concepts in a model or solution.
- **Creation boss:** compose, write, design, program, or build under constraints.
- **Explanation boss:** defend a claim, strategy, interpretation, or model.
- **Retrieval boss:** return after time without reteaching.
- **Transfer boss:** use knowledge in a changed representation or unfamiliar
  context.
- **Cross-world boss:** integrate disciplines without weakening any construct.

### Always-open rules

- Every unit boss appears on the map when the unit appears.
- The door shows outcomes tested, estimated challenge, optional preparation,
  accessibility, and approximate length.
- The child may enter, leave, or retry without penalty.
- The first attempt does not reveal every answer if the boss will be reused for
  official evidence.
- Alternate forms prevent memorized click paths from becoming mastery.
- Passing awards credit for the outcomes directly sampled.
- Incomplete prerequisite evidence produces short confirmation encounters.
- A failed attempt becomes a private diagnostic map and training suggestion.
- Boss success may change story recognition and unlock deeper territory but
  never blocks return to ordinary play.

### Boss emotional design

The boss can feel dramatic without implying that mistakes are dangerous to the
child.

- The world stakes are fictional and humorous.
- The player may preview sensory intensity.
- No jump scares, humiliating voice lines, countdown pressure, or loss of saved
  possessions.
- Failure animation shows the system consequence, not the avatar being harmed
  or ridiculed.
- A character may model strategic retreat.
- The retry screen offers “Try again,” “Train,” “Change mode,” “Explain what
  happened,” and “Leave for now.”

## 11. Failure and feedback

### Feedback order

1. Show the consequence of the action.
2. Ask the learner to notice what happened.
3. Identify the relevant relationship if needed.
4. Offer the smallest useful hint.
5. Re-present through another valid model if the same error persists.
6. Provide a worked example when productive struggle has ended.
7. Return to a near case, then the original or alternate case.

### Humor

Humor comes from characters, systems, timing, absurd stakes, and literal
consequences. The child's error is never the joke.

Examples:

- A missing rest makes every creature dance through lunch.
- An unequal equation tilts a moon café and slowly rolls dumplings downhill.
- Ambiguous punctuation causes a robot to invite the storm inside for tea.
- A magnet refuses a banana with excessive professional dignity.
- A Spanish character receives twelve onions because the tiny antagonist
  panicked and repeated the wrong order.

### Hint taxonomy

- replay instruction;
- define word;
- highlight relevant object;
- remind goal;
- show earlier analogous case;
- reveal one step;
- restore concrete model;
- reduce irrelevant choices;
- model think-aloud;
- provide full worked example; and
- suggest prerequisite training.

Only hints that materially affect independence are recorded in mastery evidence.

## 12. Rewards, avatar, and progression

### Three currencies of meaning

1. **Adventure Points:** long-term avatar level and world recognition.
2. **Mastery Sparks:** skill-specific evidence, never spendable.
3. **Artifacts:** the child's actual creations and best work.

### Reward principles

- Recognize demonstrated learning, brave attempts, revision, creation, and
  helpful explanation.
- Cap rewards for mechanical repetition.
- Never remove earned points for academic errors.
- Never sell points or mastery.
- Never tie academic power to paid cosmetics.
- Avoid random-reward loot mechanics.
- Keep rewards visible but secondary during deep play.

### K–2 avatar unlocks

- clothing colors, patterns, jackets, capes, boots, pajamas, formal outfits,
  science gear, music gear, and culturally grounded items with context;
- hairstyles, assistive devices, glasses, hats, and accessories;
- ship/room themes, shelves, display cases, gardens, and workshop tools;
- instruments and harmless companion creatures;
- poses, emotes, trails, and celebration animations; and
- subject tools that evolve visually without labelling fixed ability.

### No leaderboard

Robby and Tommy may visit one another's private galleries or collaborate with
parent approval. The platform does not rank total points, reading speed,
correct-answer streak, or grade level.

## 13. Portfolio capture

### Automatic candidate artifacts

- final and revised writing;
- decodable reading or oral explanation when parent-approved;
- mathematical model and explanation;
- science prediction, data, initial model, and revised model;
- map/source claim;
- Spanish performance or dialogue;
- music loop, score, and performance;
- code/making project; and
- boss demonstration.

### Capture rules

- Save academic state, not just a decorative screenshot.
- Include learner, date, activity/version, outcome, difficulty, supports,
  evidence state, and standards.
- Preserve revision lineage.
- Let the child and parent star work independently.
- Generate a visual “binder page” from structured evidence.
- Permit parent notes and offline photographs.
- Keep raw practice out of the best-work gallery unless chosen.
- Export in human-readable portfolio form and protected structured backup.

### Screenshot design

Every major creation and boss result has a deliberate portfolio camera state:
clean framing, readable work, avatar optional, date/title space, and no transient
UI clutter. The screenshot supports inspection but never replaces underlying
evidence.

## 14. Parent layer

The child sees story, action, and creation. The parent sees:

- today's recommended missions and why;
- optional bosses and what they assess;
- pacing against the flexible year map;
- standards and outcome coverage;
- evidence state and later retrieval date;
- supports used and misconception notes;
- suggested offline conversation or material;
- linked textbook/video supplement with preview and fallback;
- screen-time estimate;
- portfolio candidates;
- source/license record where relevant; and
- authoring/review version.

Parent controls include:

- TTS and accessibility preferences;
- motion, audio, and sensory settings;
- community entirely off by default in the current private version;
- external video/link permission;
- difficulty recommendation behavior;
- recording/media consent;
- data export, correction, and deletion; and
- the ability to mark an activity interrupted without treating it as failure.

## 15. Read-along textbook integration

Every subject world has a calm optional book portal.

### Book modes

- **Read:** clean page with adjustable typography.
- **Listen:** natural human or carefully selected natural voice, with sentence
  highlighting.
- **Explore:** tap diagrams, vocabulary, audio, and small simulations.
- **Print:** stable PDF-friendly layout.
- **Jump to mission:** enter the activity associated with a concept.
- **Return from mission:** reopen at the relevant explanation or worked example.

The book never auto-scrolls or rewards time spent staring at it. A child may
read ahead freely. Reading ahead does not auto-award mastery; the Boss Door
allows immediate demonstration.

---

## 16. Video integration

Videos appear as curated objects inside a lesson, not embedded entertainment
feeds.

### Video encounter

1. Character poses a question or the child observes a still/short teaser.
2. Child predicts where appropriate.
3. Only the selected segment plays.
4. Playback stops at the planned point.
5. Child acts on, models, or explains what was observed.
6. Source and optional full link appear in the parent/resource layer.

No autoplay, recommendation sidebar, comments, ads, or child account is
required in the core interface. When safe embedding cannot provide that
experience, open the resource through a parent-mediated external link or use
the fallback.

---

## 17. Offline bridge design

The platform frequently asks the learner to leave the screen.

### Bridge sequence

1. Show a short material list with substitutions.
2. Demonstrate safety and the first step.
3. Enter “Field Mode” with a large timer, audio prompt, or printable card only
   if useful.
4. Child reads, builds, moves, measures, observes, interviews, cooks, draws, or
   plays away from the screen.
5. Return through a simple observation, photo, drawing, measurement, parent
   note, or explanation.
6. Use the result in the on-screen model or story.

### Offline evidence

Parent confirmation is a valid evidence source when the activity is inherently
physical. The record distinguishes parent observation from system-scored or
reviewer-scored evidence.

## 18. Three.js and WebGL role

Three.js/WebGL is used when spatial, physical, systemic, or atmospheric
representation materially improves learning or wonder.

### Strong uses

- word/phoneme objects that visibly transform;
- balance, quantity, measurement, and geometric construction;
- force, motion, light, sound, circuit, magnetic-field, sky, and land models;
- navigable story/event arenas;
- musical environments animated by score and form;
- maps, timelines, habitats, machines, and avatar/home spaces;
- boss-scale spectacle tied directly to the governing concept; and
- world changes reflecting long-term progress.

### Weak uses

- 3D menus that make selection harder;
- decorative particles that obscure text or controls;
- avatar walking required to reach every short activity;
- camera motion unrelated to meaning;
- physics whose behavior contradicts the taught model;
- visual spectacle that reveals the answer; and
- inaccessible interactions with no semantic equivalent.

### Progressive enhancement

- Core task state lives outside the rendered scene.
- Keyboard focus and semantic controls mirror 3D objects.
- Reduced-motion and 2D modes retain the academic relationship.
- Low-power mode reduces particles, shadows, post-processing, and scene
  complexity.
- The lesson remains recoverable after rendering failure.
- Portfolio evidence is generated from structured state, not only canvas pixels.

## 19. Audio and voice

### English narration

- natural, warm, calm, non-condescending voice;
- short instructions with replay;
- character performances distinct from instructional narration;
- pronunciation reviewed for phonics accuracy;
- no exaggerated “baby” prosody;
- audio control per category: narration, dialogue, effects, music; and
- downloadable/local asset strategy for essential core audio where licensing
  permits.

### Spanish

- proficient human speakers are the primary models;
- region/country and speaker context recorded internally;
- more than one appropriate voice over time;
- TTS used only as fallback or draft support;
- slower replay may be derived carefully without unnatural pitch artifacts;
  natural speed remains available; and
- transcripts and visual meaning support every clip.

### Music

Sound latency must be low enough for playful composition. A sound may not claim
to represent an instrument or pitch inaccurately. Visual animation follows
audio timing rather than creating a conflicting beat.

---

## 20. Accessibility and sensory design

### Required from first prototype

- keyboard-complete operation;
- visible focus;
- screen-reader labels and semantic task equivalent;
- captions/transcripts;
- adjustable text size/spacing where practical;
- color-independent encoding;
- reduced-motion mode;
- audio-only information represented visually;
- visual-only information described or represented tactually/offline where
  practical;
- no required drag without select-and-place alternative;
- no required speed unless timing is the construct;
- pause at any time;
- predictable navigation and undo; and
- clear separation between accessibility support and answer-revealing support.

### Sensory intensity

The player can preview and lower:

- flashes and contrast changes;
- camera movement;
- particles;
- overlapping sounds;
- bass/intense effects;
- character chatter; and
- boss drama.

No essential information depends on sudden sensory events.

---

## 21. Evidence and telemetry

Collect only what supports learning, safety, reliability, and family records.

### Academic event model

- learner and local profile identifier;
- lesson/task/version;
- skill/outcome;
- timestamp and elapsed active time with uncertainty;
- difficulty and representation;
- response/model state;
- meaningful hints/supports;
- correction path;
- completion/evidence state;
- artifact reference;
- later retrieval target; and
- parent/reviewer observation.

### Product-quality events

- crashes and performance failures;
- inaccessible or abandoned interaction point;
- repeated instruction replay;
- accidental input/undo patterns;
- voluntary free-play continuation;
- return to a saved creation; and
- external-resource/fallback availability.

Do not collect biometric emotion inference, advertising profiles, unrelated
browsing, contact graphs, precise location, or covert recordings.

### Interpretation caution

Time on task may mean engagement, confusion, distraction, accessibility
friction, or deep creation. Click count may mean exploration or interface
failure. No metric becomes an educational conclusion without context and direct
evidence.

---

## 22. Content authoring contract

Every playable lesson package declares:

- internal ID, version, subject, world, and arc;
- learner lane and grade reporting range;
- academic outcome and standards;
- governing relationship;
- prerequisites and misconceptions;
- player fantasy and narrative beat;
- universal verbs used;
- 3–5 learn/practice sections;
- representations and transitions;
- Normal/Hard/Master transformations;
- Boss Door assessment and exact evidence boundary;
- hint ladder;
- failure consequences and recovery;
- free play/creation;
- Window Upward;
- offline bridge;
- textbook and media links;
- accessibility alternatives;
- artifact/portfolio definition;
- retrieval schedule;
- source/license records;
- privacy/safety needs;
- parent guidance; and
- review and pilot history.

The package cannot write directly to official mastery. It submits evidence to
the learning-record rules, which determine the evidence state transparently.

## 23. Worked vertical slice A: Robby's Short-A Workshop

### Academic outcome

Hear, segment, map, blend, read, and encode short-`a` CVC words using taught
consonants; read a controlled sentence independently.

### Story

A hungry alien's snack machine changes one sound at a time and keeps producing
increasingly useless objects instead of food.

### Sections

1. **Sound Kitchen:** hear `/ă/`; sort spoken words by whether the middle sound
   matches; no print yet.
2. **Pod Recipe:** move one pod per phoneme; attach letters after oral
   segmentation.
3. **Word Reactor:** transform `at → sat → Sam → mat`; watch meaning change.
4. **Decodable Delivery:** read a short controlled scene; the alien acts only
   after the attempt.
5. **Snack Boss:** spell two words from sound and read an unfamiliar cumulative
   sentence.

### Difficulty

- Normal: mouth/sound reminder, pods, letters, guided blend rail.
- Hard: fewer cues, more substitution, spelling from human audio.
- Master: unfamiliar decodable transfer and correction of a plausible
  misspelling—never an untaught pattern.

### Free play

Create a valid short-`a` creature or object name from taught graphemes, draw it,
and record a decodable label/caption.

### Evidence boundary

The boss demonstrates only the sound-spellings, segmentation, blending,
decoding, and encoding sampled. Listening to the rich story does not count as
independent reading.

### Retrieval

Next session, 3–5 days, festival week, then embedded inside a later mixed-vowel
mission.

## 24. Worked vertical slice B: Tommy's Storm Core Trial

### Academic outcome

Explain plot causality and character response, make an inference, and support a
claim with relevant textual evidence in an original high-velocity story.

### Story

Destroying the storm core may save the city from one threat while removing the
shield protecting it from another. The rival has already begun a dramatic
countdown despite having no authority to do so.

### Sections

1. **Cold Open:** predict the consequence of the rival's plan from a short scene.
2. **Rapid Read:** read the original episode with optional glossary and natural
   read-aloud available outside independent-reading evidence.
3. **Story Arena:** reconstruct cause, action, response, and consequence.
4. **Evidence Core:** attach two details to a claim and reject a true but
   irrelevant detail.
5. **Storm Boss:** defend a conclusion independently; Master adds a conflicting
   witness or implied-theme transfer.

### Difficulty

- Normal: chunked passage, glossary, scene cards, explicit evidence candidates.
- Hard: reduced chunking, constructed cause/motive response, distractor
  evidence.
- Master: conflicting viewpoint, implied theme or foreshadowing, and two-detail
  defense in an unfamiliar scene.

### Free play

Rewrite or stage the choice, change one piece of evidence, and observe how the
defensible conclusion changes.

### Evidence boundary

TTS-supported comprehension is valid listening/content evidence but not
independent decoding or oral-reading evidence. The system stores those claims
separately.

## 25. Worked shared slice: Force Frontier rescue course

### Shared phenomenon

A snack capsule must move through a rescue course using pushes, pulls,
collisions, slopes, and magnets without being carried directly.

### Robby evidence

- predict motion from a push or pull;
- compare strength/direction qualitatively;
- observe and describe what changed;
- revise one part of the course; and
- sort magnetic interactions from non-interactions without claiming all metal
  is magnetic.

### Tommy evidence

- represent force direction;
- design a fair comparison;
- measure distance or repeated outcome;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- build a causal model; and
- defend a revised design from evidence.

### Shared free play

Build the funniest successful course, then explain why it works. Complexity and
spectacle do not replace the grade-specific evidence.

## 26. Visual and animation direction for K–2

### Form language

- readable silhouettes;
- expressive faces and poses;
- tactile toy-like academic objects with precise state changes;
- bold but controlled color zones;
- quiet backgrounds behind text and evidence;
- dramatic scale reserved for phenomena, bosses, and earned reveals;
- 2D illustrated panels integrated with 3D spaces; and
- materials that feel warm, crafted, luminous, and slightly strange rather
  than generic glossy ed-tech plastic.

### Animation priorities

1. academic state change;
2. input acknowledgment;
3. character reaction;
4. world consequence;
5. reward flourish.

Animation cannot delay repeated practice. Reactions shorten or become
skippable after first viewing.

### Age-resilient style

Avoid preschool visual clichés that Tommy will reject and Robby does not need.
Use an animated adventure tone with humor, graphic shapes, mysterious machines,
natural environments, and genuine dramatic composition. The same world should
be capable of visually maturing across later grades.

## 27. Performance budgets

Initial production targets should include:

- meaningful interaction quickly after scene load;
- graceful operation on ordinary family laptops and tablets;
- low-power and 2D modes;
- downloadable/cacheable core assets where appropriate;
- lazy loading by world/mission;
- no essential lesson blocked by a large decorative asset;
- recoverable save after every meaningful section;
- deterministic restoration of an interrupted task; and
- audio preloading sufficient for responsive music and phonics interaction.

Exact frame-rate, memory, bundle-size, and latency budgets are set after the
supported-device inventory is documented and prototypes are measured.

## 28. Quality gates

### Curriculum gate

- outcome, prerequisite, sequence, and standard are accurate;
- practice amount and variation are sufficient;
- boss evidence matches the claim;
- later retrieval exists; and
- source/content review is complete.

### Game gate

- the first meaningful action is clear;
- mechanic embodies the relationship;
- feedback is causal and useful;
- failure is safe;
- choice or experimentation exists;
- free play remains academically connected; and
- replay has meaning.

### Child-experience gate

- humor and character desire are present;
- instruction is not needlessly verbal;
- no waiting or animation tax dominates practice;
- challenge can be changed without shame;
- boss can be attempted immediately;
- stopping points are healthy; and
- the learner can create or wonder beyond completion.

### Accessibility gate

- keyboard, focus, semantic alternative, reduced motion, captions/transcripts,
  color redundancy, non-drag input, pause, and 2D fallback pass review; and
- supports do not invalidate the assessment claim.

### Evidence gate

- task state, difficulty, meaningful supports, response, artifact, version, and
  date are captured;
- portfolio output is inspectable;
- no decorative metric is treated as mastery; and
- privacy/data minimization is satisfied.

### Technical gate

- supported devices perform acceptably;
- interruption and restoration work;
- offline/external fallbacks work;
- no console/runtime errors affect the learning path;
- asset and source licenses are recorded; and
- test coverage matches risk.

## 29. Playtest protocol

Observe rather than over-explain.

### Watch for

- whether the child knows the first action;
- where adult help becomes necessary;
- whether the child uses the intended academic relationship;
- false positives from guessing, pictures, interface cues, or repeated paths;
- frustration versus productive struggle;
- whether feedback changes the next attempt;
- voluntary replay or creation after required learning;
- fatigue and healthy stopping;
- accessibility friction;
- what the child later remembers or transfers; and
- whether the portfolio record matches what actually happened.

### Ask afterward

- “What were you trying to do?”
- “What changed when you did that?”
- “How did you know?”
- “What would you try next?”
- “Was anything confusing or boring?”
- “What would make you come back?”

Do not ask leading questions that teach the target during an independent test.

## 30. Production order

1. Build the shared interaction grammar and accessible object manipulation.
2. Build learner switching, local records, evidence states, and portfolio
   capture before scaling content.
3. Prototype Robby's Short-A Workshop.
4. Prototype Tommy's Storm Core Trial using the same lesson shell but different
   mechanics/evidence.
5. Prototype the shared comparison/equality mathematics machine.
6. Prototype the shared Force Frontier rescue course with differentiated
   evidence.
7. Replace the music proof-of-concept with the planned beat/pitch/loop lab and
   save library.
8. Build the first Spanish human-audio social scene.
9. Playtest each vertical slice, revise the engine, and only then produce full
   units.
10. Add avatar wardrobe and home-gallery improvements alongside earned
    artifacts.
11. Keep public community and mod-library surfaces disabled until their
    dedicated safety, privacy, moderation, and governance gates are ready.

The first production milestone is not “all K–2 content.” It is one complete,
auditable, delightful vertical slice per core mechanic family, with evidence
that children learn and voluntarily continue playing.
