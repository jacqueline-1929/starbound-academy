# Starbound Avatar, Community, and Mod Ecosystem

**Version:** 0.1 — future-system design  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Status:** Architecture only; the current private family platform has no public
community or marketplace.

> Build a commons, not a feed: a place to make, exhibit, teach, and respond
without turning childhood into content or attention into a commodity.

This document defines three connected long-term systems:

1. a persistent avatar and personal space;
2. a safe, asynchronous learning community; and
3. a reviewed mod library for community-created lessons, worlds, and tools.

It should be read with the
[World, Experience, and Pedagogy Design Bible](STARBOUND_ACADEMY_DESIGN_BIBLE.md)
and the
[Accreditation and Academic Quality Framework](ACCREDITATION_AND_ACADEMIC_QUALITY_FRAMEWORK.md).

---

## 1. Non-negotiable principles

1. **No advertising.** No behavioral ads, sponsored placement, product
   placement, or sale of learner attention or data.
2. **No social-media machinery.** No infinite feed, follower counts, public
   popularity score, engagement bait, disappearing posts, streak pressure, or
   recommendation system optimized for time-on-platform.
3. **Asynchronous by design.** Community activity happens through exhibits,
   workshops, prompts, critique, and correspondence that do not demand instant
   response.
4. **Children share work, not personal lives.** The platform centers stories,
   music, experiments, designs, explanations, and lessons—not selfies,
   locations, daily status, or personal audience building.
5. **The smallest safe audience wins.** Private and family spaces are defaults;
   classrooms, invitation groups, and moderated public exhibitions are
   deliberate expansions.
6. **Creation outranks consumption.** The system helps a learner make,
   document, revise, teach, and respond before it offers more things to browse.
7. **Feedback must be useful.** Responses are structured around noticing,
   questioning, evidence, appreciation, and constructive suggestions.
8. **No private child-to-child messaging.** Communication occurs in visible,
   bounded, reviewable spaces with age-appropriate controls.
9. **Academic authority stays explicit.** Community content is not official
   Starbound curriculum merely because it is popular or polished.
10. **Safety and privacy are product architecture.** They cannot depend on a
    warning page or heroic moderation after launch.

## 2. The persistent avatar

The avatar grows with the learner across years. It represents identity,
adventure, creation, and contribution—not academic rank, wealth, or popularity.

### Customization

- body shapes and proportions that remain stylized rather than photorealistic;
- skin tones, facial features, hair, textures, colors, and assistive devices;
- clothes, armor, uniforms, costumes, formalwear, sleepwear, and absurd outfits;
- shoes, hats, glasses, masks, capes, bags, jewelry, tools, instruments, and
  scientific equipment;
- color palettes, patterns, patches, and layered styling;
- poses, movement styles, emotes, and victory animations;
- rooms, ships, studios, gardens, libraries, laboratories, and display walls;
- pets or companions with non-disruptive comic behavior; and
- style presets that can mature from playful animation into graphic-novel,
  science-fiction, historical, artistic, and minimalist aesthetics.

Body options must avoid ranking bodies, gender policing, sexualization, or
tying physical traits to academic abilities. Clothing is not divided into
mandatory “boy” and “girl” inventories.

### How items are earned

Items may recognize:

- completing meaningful learning or a long project;
- demonstrating durable mastery;
- attempting a boss before feeling ready;
- revising a creation;
- contributing useful feedback;
- participating in an exhibition;
- helping test or improve an accessible design; and
- discovering a hidden idea or historical/artistic connection.

No item is earned by watching an advertisement, purchasing a loot box,
maintaining an anxiety-producing streak, farming empty clicks, or defeating
another child's status.

### Wardrobe and loadouts

- Save named outfits for different worlds or performances.
- Preview every item before equipping it.
- Permit remixing colors and patterns without artificial scarcity.
- Include a child-safe randomizer and an undo history.
- Keep functional learning supports independent of cosmetics.
- Allow private outfits that never need to be shared.
- Preserve earned items if a learner changes grade, difficulty, or community
  participation.

### Personal gallery

The avatar's home base is a navigable portfolio:

- a music wall for saved loops and performances;
- a bookshelf for stories, research, and read-along texts;
- a laboratory for models, investigations, and field notes;
- a workshop for mathematics, code, engineering, and mods;
- an exhibition wall for child- and parent-starred work; and
- a timeline that shows growth without reducing it to one score.

The private gallery exists before any community feature. Sharing is a separate,
reversible decision for each artifact.

## 3. The Starbound Commons

The community should feel like a library, museum, studio, science fair, maker
space, and correspondence club—not a social network.

### Primary places

| Place | Purpose | Typical interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family Gallery | Private portfolio and discussion | Parent/child notes and starring |
| Crew Room | Small invitation-only learning group | Shared prompt, critique, collaboration |
| Exhibition Hall | Moderated showcase organized by subject or theme | Browse a finite exhibit and leave structured responses |
| Listening Room | Music and audio creations | Listen, study the score, remix only when permitted |
| Reading Table | Stories, comics, essays, and research | Read, annotate privately, offer guided feedback |
| Science Fair | Questions, methods, models, evidence, and revisions | Ask about variables, evidence, and next tests |
| Maker Dock | Code, designs, simulations, and build instructions | Inspect, test, fork with attribution |
| Lesson Library | Reviewed community-created learning mods | Preview, inspect, install, rate against a rubric |

There is no global home feed. A learner enters a named place for a named
purpose and sees a finite, intentionally ordered collection.

### Audience levels

Every share has one explicit audience:

1. only me;
2. family;
3. named mentor;
4. invitation-only crew or class;
5. moderated exhibition; or
6. public creator library, available only for eligible adult-managed creator
   accounts and reviewed packages.

Changing audience requires an affirmative action. The system never silently
turns private work public.

### Structured responses

Young learners begin with response tools such as:

- “I noticed…”
- “I wondered…”
- “My favorite choice was… because…”
- “What happened when you changed…?”
- “The evidence that convinced me was…”
- “Would you like one idea to try?”
- subject-specific response cards for rhythm, plot, mathematical models,
  scientific variables, language use, art, and code.

Free text expands gradually with age, context, moderation capacity, and parent
settings. Reactions, if used, describe the work—“clear explanation,” “surprising
idea,” “strong rhythm,” or “I tried this”—rather than assign a public popularity
number to the child.

### Deliberate friction

Good friction protects attention and improves response quality:

- finite exhibits rather than endless scrolling;
- no autoplay;
- no push notification for every reaction;
- batched community summaries at a parent-selected time;
- prompts to listen/read/view before responding;
- a cooling-off and edit period for written critique;
- daily response limits for younger accounts;
- no “seen” receipts or pressure for immediate reply; and
- natural stopping points that invite the learner back to making or offline
  activity.

Success is measured by meaningful creations, revisions, useful responses,
learning transfer, and healthy return—not daily active minutes.

## 4. Child safety and privacy architecture

Before any community launch, Starbound requires a dedicated legal, privacy,
security, moderation, and child-safety review for the jurisdictions served.

### Identity boundaries

- child-facing pseudonym by default;
- no required public real name, birth date, school, email, precise location,
  schedule, or contact information;
- separate verified parent/guardian administration;
- age-band capabilities without exposing exact age publicly;
- automatic detection and blocking of common personal-information patterns;
- metadata removal from uploaded media;
- no discoverability through phone contacts or external social graphs; and
- no public list of a child's community relationships.

### Communication boundaries

- no open direct messages between minors;
- no adult-to-child private messaging outside an explicitly authorized,
  auditable mentorship context;
- no image, link, file, audio, or video upload until that media type has an
  adequate safety pipeline;
- parent controls for sharing, audiences, commenting, remixing, and community
  participation;
- visible reporting and blocking tools;
- human escalation for ambiguous or serious cases; and
- documented emergency, abuse, self-harm, grooming, threat, and law-enforcement
  response procedures before scale.

Automated moderation may assist triage. It never replaces trained human review,
appeals, audit, or age-appropriate support.

### Media defaults

For younger learners, share the artifact rather than the child:

- synthesized or parent-approved narration instead of a child's identifying
  voice where practical;
- avatar performance instead of face video;
- cropped project photography with background review;
- no live location, livestreaming, or unmoderated live chat; and
- parent preview before broader publication.

### Data posture

- no ads or cross-service tracking;
- no sale of personal data;
- no training unrelated models on child work without a separate lawful,
  understandable, affirmative process;
- minimal retention and clear deletion controls;
- export of the child's work and feedback;
- audience and moderation audit logs;
- security incident response; and
- independent privacy and security review before public community use.

## 5. Moderation and community governance

### Layered moderation

1. **Design prevention:** bounded audiences, structured responses, limited
   media, no DMs, and no virality.
2. **Automated screening:** personal information, abuse, sexual content,
   threats, spam, malware, unsafe links, and evasion patterns.
3. **Human review:** publication queues, reports, ambiguous cases, creator mods,
   and appeals.
4. **Parent and educator control:** approve audiences, remove work, restrict
   contact, and inspect relevant history.
5. **Governance review:** publish rules, measure errors, review disparities,
   and revise policy.

### Enforcement should teach when safe

Minor mistakes may receive a private explanation and chance to edit. Serious or
predatory behavior requires protective action, preservation of relevant
evidence, and escalation. The system never asks a child to personally mediate a
safety violation.

### Appeals and transparency

- explain the rule and action in age-appropriate language;
- allow an adult-managed appeal;
- preserve the original and decision record securely;
- distinguish safety removal from academic criticism;
- publish aggregate transparency information when the community reaches scale;
  and
- audit moderation for systematic errors and unequal impact.

## 6. The Mod Library

“Mod store” is the familiar internal concept. The child-facing experience is a
curated **Lesson Library** or **Creator Workshop**, not a commercial app-store
feed.

Mods may include:

- lessons and practice sets;
- boss challenges;
- original stories and read-along books;
- science investigations and simulations;
- mathematics models and puzzles;
- Spanish dialogues and human-recorded audio with permission;
- music instruments, notation challenges, and loop packs;
- avatar clothing and room objects;
- maps, quests, characters, and world decorations;
- accessibility tools; and
- teacher/parent dashboards and export templates.

### Package requirements

Every mod has a machine-readable manifest:

- creator and responsible adult/entity;
- version, release date, and change log;
- intended age/grade and prerequisites;
- learning objectives and claimed standards;
- instructional and assessment design;
- estimated time and required materials;
- accessibility features and known barriers;
- data accessed, stored, or transmitted;
- permissions requested;
- external links, services, and dependencies;
- source and asset licenses;
- content advisories;
- moderation and academic review status;
- whether AI assisted creation and how outputs were reviewed; and
- compatibility and safe-uninstall information.

### Sandboxing

A mod receives no access to learner identity, portfolio, messages, mastery
record, microphone, camera, location, network, or files unless that permission
is necessary, declared, narrowly scoped, parent-approved, and technically
enforced.

Most lesson mods should consume anonymized session inputs and return candidate
evidence through a controlled Starbound interface. They should not directly
edit official mastery or transcript records.

### Academic trust labels

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Community experiment | Safety-reviewed for a limited audience; no academic endorsement |
| Creator-published | Identity/account and package checks complete; claims remain the creator's |
| Starbound-reviewed | Subject, pedagogy, assessment, accessibility, source, and safety review complete |
| Program-approved | Accepted into a named Starbound course version through formal academic governance |
| Partner-approved | Approved under the policies of a future authorized school or college partner |

Popularity can never promote a mod into an academic trust category.

Community mods may enrich learning and generate portfolio artifacts. Only a
program-approved version can satisfy official Starbound curriculum requirements,
and only an authorized institution can decide official school or college
credit.

### Discovery without an engagement feed

Mods are discovered through:

- librarian/editor collections;
- subject, concept, age, accessibility, language, material, and duration
  filters;
- prerequisite and standards maps;
- “works with this lesson” links chosen by curriculum reviewers;
- finite seasonal exhibitions;
- parent or teacher recommendations; and
- transparent quality signals such as review status, update recency,
  accessibility, successful completion, and documented learning evidence.

No paid placement, hidden ranking purchase, engagement-optimized recommendation,
or child-targeted promotion is permitted.

### Reviews

Reviews evaluate the artifact, not the creator's social status:

- Was the objective clear?
- Did the activity work as described?
- What did the learner create or understand?
- Were instructions and accessibility sufficient?
- Did the assessment match the objective?
- Were there technical, safety, source, or accuracy concerns?

Public star counts and competitive download leaderboards are avoided. Review
summaries may show rubric evidence and known issues without manufacturing a
popularity contest.

## 7. Creator participation and economics

The initial library should be free, invitation-based, and deliberately small.
If paid mods are introduced later:

- adults or verified legal entities handle creator accounts and payment;
- children are never pressured to monetize schoolwork;
- prices, platform share, refunds, taxes, licenses, and update obligations are
  plain;
- no loot boxes, gambling mechanics, tips directed at minors, artificial
  scarcity, or pay-to-win academic advantage;
- scholarships or family-library access prevent core education from depending
  on repeated purchases;
- required official curriculum is never quietly moved behind third-party
  purchases;
- paid status never affects search prominence or academic approval;
- creators retain appropriate rights while granting necessary delivery and
  review permissions; and
- abandoned paid mods have a continuity and refund policy.

The word “store” must never turn the academy into a mall.

## 8. Remixing and attribution

Remixing is educational when authorship remains visible.

- Creators choose whether an artifact may be remixed.
- Forks retain parent-work attribution and license information.
- The system displays what changed.
- Child work is not eligible for public commercial remix by default.
- Public-domain and open-license materials retain required notices.
- Copyright complaints and counter-notice processes exist before public scale.
- A remix cannot inherit academic approval automatically; material changes
  trigger review.
- AI-generated or AI-assisted components are labelled according to policy and
  reviewed for accuracy, rights, bias, and safety.

## 9. Community learning evidence

Community activity may produce evidence such as:

- publishing a revised work for a real audience;
- explaining a mathematical or scientific choice;
- giving subject-specific critique;
- responding thoughtfully to a question;
- collaborating with a defined role and contribution record;
- documenting a remix and attribution chain; or
- teaching a concept through a reviewed mod.

Community participation itself is never required to prove an academic outcome
that can be demonstrated privately. A child who does not share publicly must
receive an equally valid route to mastery and a complete education.

Likes, views, downloads, followers, response count, and virality are not
academic evidence.

## 10. Age and capability progression

| Stage | Typical community capability |
| --- | --- |
| K–2 | Private gallery; family sharing; parent-approved class exhibit; structured response cards; no public profile |
| 3–5 | Invitation crews; moderated themed exhibits; guided remixing; limited free text with review |
| 6–8 | Project studios; longer critique; collaborative roles; supervised creator tools; source and license instruction |
| 9–12 | Cross-school exhibitions; mentorship under policy; substantial mods; editorial and moderation apprenticeships |
| A.A. stage/adult | Public creator identity by choice; advanced review; research and open-source collaboration; eligible marketplace participation |

Age is not the only control. Parent choice, account history, jurisdiction,
moderation capacity, media type, and the specific risk determine capability.

## 11. Success and failure measures

### Healthy success

- learners create more than they browse;
- shared work receives specific, kind, useful responses;
- critique produces visible revision;
- community participation improves transfer and belonging;
- parents understand and trust audience controls;
- safety reports are handled accurately and quickly;
- high-quality accessible mods are discoverable without popularity gaming;
- creators maintain and improve their work; and
- learners stop at natural points without compulsion.

### Warning signs

- browsing time rises while creation falls;
- children ask about follower or popularity status;
- response pressure disrupts lessons, sleep, or offline life;
- appearance items become the dominant reason to perform academic work;
- public sharing becomes necessary for status or curriculum completion;
- low-quality mods win through novelty or promotion;
- moderators cannot review the media or volume being accepted;
- adults seek private access to children;
- creators collect undeclared data; or
- academic approval is confused with community popularity.

Any warning sign may justify slowing, narrowing, or disabling a feature. Growth
is not a safety argument.

## 12. Build order

1. **Private identity:** robust avatar, wardrobe, room, and private portfolio.
2. **Family gallery:** parent/child sharing, comments, starring, and export.
3. **Local creator tools:** build and install private family mods with manifests
   and sandboxing.
4. **Invitation crews:** small adult-managed groups with structured responses.
5. **Moderated exhibitions:** finite, reviewed, theme-based public showcases.
6. **Reviewed Lesson Library:** invited adult creators and formal trust labels.
7. **Broader creator ecosystem:** only after independent child-safety,
   privacy, security, accessibility, academic, and operational review.
8. **Optional paid library:** only after separate economic, legal, equity, tax,
   refund, moderation, and child-protection design.

The architecture should support the later stages without exposing unfinished
community surfaces early.
