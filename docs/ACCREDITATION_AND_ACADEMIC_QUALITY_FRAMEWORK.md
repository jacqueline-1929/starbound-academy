# Starbound Academy Accreditation and Academic Quality Framework

**Version:** 0.1 — planning framework  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Status:** Design target; Starbound Academy is not currently an accredited
institution and does not currently award college credit or degrees.

> Build every course as if a skeptical external reviewer will inspect the
> objective, instructor, source, assessment, student work, result, and revision.

This framework establishes the level of scrutiny Starbound will use while it is
still a private family platform. It does not grant accreditation. It creates the
records, quality processes, and institutional habits that would make a future
application or partnership credible.

It should be read with:

- the [World, Experience, and Pedagogy Design Bible](STARBOUND_ACADEMY_DESIGN_BIBLE.md);
- the [K–12 and Associate of Arts Education Plan](K14_EDUCATION_PLAN.md);

- the [K–2 Game Design Specification](K2_GAME_DESIGN_SPECIFICATION.md);
- the [Avatar, Community, and Mod Ecosystem](AVATAR_COMMUNITY_AND_MOD_ECOSYSTEM.md); and
- future course specifications and standards crosswalks.

## 1. The claim ladder

Starbound must use precise language. These claims are not interchangeable.

| Level | Meaning | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research-informed | Design decisions cite credible research or official frameworks | In development |
| Standards-mapped | Objectives and assessments are crosswalked to named, versioned standards | Partial |
| Curriculum-complete | A documented sequence contains instruction, practice, assessment, materials, accessibility, and teacher guidance | Not yet |
| Independently reviewed | Qualified external reviewers have examined content, alignment, accessibility, and assessment | Not yet |
| Field-tested | Learner evidence shows usability, engagement, learning, retention, and transfer | Early family testing only |
| K–12 accredited | A selected independent accreditor has evaluated an eligible school or institution | Not accredited |
| College-equivalent rigor | Lower-division courses meet documented college-level outcomes, workload, faculty, and assessment expectations | Design target |
| Credit-bearing | An authorized institution awards transcripted credit | Not authorized |
| A.A.-granting | A legally authorized institution awards an Associate in Arts degree | Not authorized |
| Institutionally accredited | A recognized accrediting agency has accredited the postsecondary institution | Not accredited |

No interface, transcript, marketing page, or portfolio may collapse this ladder.
Until the relevant authority exists, the product may say “A.A.-aligned design
target” or “college-equivalent portfolio pathway,” but not “accredited,”
“college credit,” or “Associate in Arts degree.”

## 2. Two different accreditation problems

### K–12

Florida does not accredit, approve, license, or regulate private K–12 schools.
Private schools may voluntarily seek accreditation from independent
organizations, whose standards and acceptance vary. Consequently, Starbound
must select a future K–12 accreditor based on the intended operating model and
verify how receiving schools, colleges, employers, scholarship programs, and
families treat that accreditation.

Curriculum quality and school accreditation are related but distinct. A
beautiful, standards-aligned platform is not automatically an accredited
school. An accredited school also requires governance, qualified personnel,
policies, student services, records, improvement processes, safety, and
operational evidence.

Starbound's K–12 intent is therefore:

> Build from the first kindergarten lesson as though the eventual school,
> curriculum, teaching system, learner evidence, and operations will be examined
> for accreditation—while making no claim that accreditation already exists.

Florida is the legal and standards floor because it is the family's home base.
It is not the definition of excellence. Starbound will also benchmark coherent
progressions, disciplinary expectations, learner agency, accessibility, and
outcomes against strong U.S. states, high-performing international systems,
respected subject frameworks, and current learning research. The final
curriculum must still explain every local requirement clearly; “world-class”
cannot become an excuse for an unmapped or missing Florida expectation.

### Postsecondary and the A.A.

The years previously called “Grades 13–14” are an internal developmental
shorthand. The actual target is a two-year **Associate in Arts stage** comparable
to the lower division of a baccalaureate program.

Florida's public A.A. model requires 60 semester credits: 36 credits of general
education and 24 credits of electives. General education spans communication,
mathematics, social sciences, humanities, and natural sciences. Transfer value
also depends on course selection, common prerequisites, institutional
authorization, articulation, and the receiving institution.

A private Florida postsecondary institution is subject to state authorization
and licensure requirements through the Commission for Independent Education
unless a specific exemption applies. Institutional accreditation is a separate
peer-review process. The U.S. Department of Education recognizes accrediting
agencies; it does not directly accredit colleges.

### Strategic conclusion

Starbound should not attempt to manufacture a degree label around family
coursework. It should develop A.A.-caliber courses and evidence while preserving
three legitimate future routes:

1. **Partner route:** an accredited college evaluates or co-develops courses
   and awards accepted credit.
2. **Articulation/dual-enrollment route:** students complete approved external
   college courses while Starbound supplies the world, tutoring, portfolio, and
   integrated projects.
3. **Institution route:** a future Starbound entity obtains Florida
   authorization, builds the required institutional capacity, and pursues
   recognized accreditation.

The partner route is likely the earliest credible route. That is a planning
judgment, not a legal conclusion.

## 3. Academic quality system

Every program operates through the same improvement cycle:

> Mission → outcomes → curriculum → instruction → evidence → analysis →
> improvement → documented follow-up.

### Institutional outcomes

Starbound will publish a small set of enduring outcomes for:

- written and oral communication;
- quantitative reasoning;
- scientific reasoning;
- historical, civic, and social analysis;
- information, media, data, and AI literacy;
- intercultural and language competence;
- creative practice;
- ethical reasoning;
- collaboration and self-direction; and
- transfer of knowledge to unfamiliar problems.

Each program and course must show where it introduces, reinforces, and assesses
these outcomes. Each outcome must have direct evidence from student work.

### K–12 program evidence

For every grade and subject, Starbound will maintain:

- a published philosophy, learner profile, and age-band outcomes;
- a complete scope and sequence with prerequisites and retrieval links;
- Florida standards crosswalks with standard version and effective dates;
- comparison crosswalks used to test depth, coherence, and omissions;
- course or unit dossiers, teacher/parent guidance, and learner materials;
- evidence that instructional time and experiences are sufficient;
- promotion, credit, grading/mastery, and graduation rules as applicable;
- valid assessments and moderated work samples;
- accessibility, intervention, and advanced-learning provisions;
- qualified author and reviewer records;
- student progress, attendance/participation, and permanent-record procedures
  appropriate to the operating model;
- annual results and documented improvements; and
- an external-review schedule.

### Beyond-compliance outcomes

Florida alignment answers, “Have we covered the required floor?” Starbound's
quality review must also ask:

- Can learners retrieve and use knowledge months and years later?
- Can they explain the model underneath a procedure?
- Can they transfer learning to a new representation or context?
- Can they read, write, investigate, calculate, create, and discuss at a level
  that compares favorably with demanding national and international work?
- Do they possess coherent historical, scientific, literary, artistic, and
  civic knowledge rather than disconnected standards fragments?
- Can they direct increasingly complex work without losing intellectual joy?
- Does the curriculum produce excellent outcomes for learners who need
  different supports, inputs, or ways to respond?

“World-class” is a hypothesis to be tested with evidence, external review, and
transparent comparisons—not a marketing adjective.

### Annual evidence cycle

1. Set a measurable learning target and acceptable threshold.
2. Collect a representative sample of direct student work.
3. Score it with a documented rubric and calibration process.
4. Disaggregate results when cohorts become large enough to do so responsibly.
5. Identify one or more changes to curriculum, teaching, support, or assessment.
6. Implement the change.
7. Measure again and document whether the change worked.

Completion rates and quiz scores are insufficient by themselves. The system
must inspect what learners can explain, create, retain, and transfer.

## 4. Course approval standard

No course is “complete” until it has an approval dossier.

### Course dossier

- stable course ID, title, level, version, and effective dates;
- catalog description and prerequisites;
- credit or instructional-time rationale where applicable;
- measurable course learning outcomes;
- alignment to program, institutional, and external outcomes;
- complete scope and sequence;
- required and optional materials with license records;
- instructional methods and rationale;
- assessment map showing direct evidence for every outcome;
- grading or mastery policy;
- workload estimate by activity type;
- accessibility and accommodation plan;
- academic integrity and AI-use expectations appropriate to age;
- learner support and intervention plan;
- instructor qualifications and reviewer approvals;
- privacy, safety, and child-protection review;
- pilot results and revision history; and
- archival copy of every released version.

### Course-level quality questions

An external reviewer should be able to answer “yes” to all of these:

1. Are the outcomes important, observable, and appropriate to the level?
2. Do the learning experiences actually prepare students for those outcomes?
3. Do assessments measure the named outcome rather than interface skill or
   compliance?
4. Is the content accurate, current where required, coherent, and sufficiently
   deep?
5. Does the course require meaningful intellectual work?
6. Are scaffolds gradually removed?
7. Can a qualified reviewer inspect representative student evidence?
8. Are accessibility supports separated from unauthorized changes to the
   construct being assessed?
9. Are sources, permissions, versions, and authorship traceable?
10. Did evidence lead to a documented improvement?

## 5. The A.A. stage

The official curriculum name should become **Starbound Associate of Arts
Stage**. “Grades 13–14” may remain only as a private timeline shorthand.

### Structural target

| Component | Design target |
| --- | ---: |
| General education | 36 semester credits |
| Transfer-oriented electives and prerequisites | 24 semester credits |
| Total | 60 semester credits |

The final distribution must be reviewed against the Florida requirements and
the intended transfer major at the time a real credential or partnership is
pursued. Laws, rules, core-course lists, and articulation agreements can
change.

### Provisional 36-credit general-education structure

| Area | Credits | Illustrative Starbound courses |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Communication | 6 | Composition and Rhetoric I; Research, Argument, and Public Communication |
| Mathematics | 6 | College Algebra/Modeling or Calculus pathway; Statistics and Quantitative Reasoning |
| Humanities | 9 | Literature and Interpretation; Philosophy and Logic; Arts and Human Meaning |
| Social sciences | 9 | U.S. Government and Civic Institutions; Economics; World History or Social Inquiry |
| Natural sciences | 6 | Two college-level sciences, with laboratory work where the course and transfer pathway require it |
| **Total** | **36** | Subject to formal review and current state/institutional requirements |

### Provisional 24-credit elective structure

Electives should not be a random collection. They form a declared exploration
or transfer pathway and may include:

- Spanish and intercultural study;
- computer science and data;
- additional laboratory science or mathematics;
- history, literature, philosophy, economics, psychology, or sociology;
- music, art, design, and performance;
- education and human development;
- engineering foundations; or
- prerequisites for the intended baccalaureate major.

At least one substantial capstone may integrate multiple courses, but it cannot
replace required disciplinary learning merely by being impressive.

### College-level course rigor

Every A.A.-stage course must demonstrate:

- college-level reading and source use;
- sustained analytical writing or another discipline-authentic equivalent;
- quantitative or evidentiary reasoning appropriate to the field;
- substantive independent work;
- instructor feedback and revision;
- cumulative assessment;
- transparent academic-integrity expectations;
- qualified faculty oversight;
- verifiable identity and authorship procedures if credit is eventually
  awarded online;
- retained representative work; and
- workload consistent with the credit awarded by the authorizing institution.

Game systems may frame, visualize, simulate, and motivate the work. They do not
reduce the intellectual standard or award credit for points, attendance, or
screen time alone.

## 6. Faculty and academic governance

Accreditation-quality curriculum cannot depend on one enthusiastic builder
without review.

### Required roles as the program grows

- governing authority with documented independence and conflict rules;
- chief academic officer or curriculum director;
- qualified subject faculty;
- assessment and institutional-effectiveness lead;
- registrar/records responsibility;
- library and learning-resource support;
- accessibility and learner-support responsibility;
- privacy, security, and child-safety responsibility; and
- external advisory reviewers.

One person may fill multiple roles during an early prototype, but the role,
decision, qualification, and conflict must be documented.

### Faculty qualification record

For every instructor, course author, and final academic reviewer, retain:

- degrees and fields;
- awarding institutions;
- licenses, certifications, professional work, publications, or artistic work;
- courses assigned;
- rationale connecting qualifications to course outcomes;
- approval decision;
- ongoing professional development; and
- periodic teaching or review evaluation.

At the A.A. stage, final course responsibility must sit with faculty whose
credentials and demonstrated expertise are appropriate to lower-division
college work. Exceptions require a written rationale and independent approval.

### Academic freedom and integrity

Starbound must protect good-faith inquiry, distinguish evidence from viewpoint,
represent consequential disagreements fairly, disclose conflicts, correct
errors publicly, and prevent commercial or narrative priorities from silently
changing academic conclusions.

## 7. Assessment validity

Every assessment begins by naming the construct.

Examples:

- A decoding assessment measures independent decoding, so full-word TTS would
  invalidate that portion.
- A science explanation measures causal reasoning, so speech-to-text may be an
  appropriate access support when writing mechanics are not the construct.
- A music composition assessment may permit replay and revision because
  iterative listening is part of authentic composition.
- A college research assessment must distinguish source discovery, synthesis,
  writing, and permitted AI assistance.

### Required evidence types

- diagnostic evidence before instruction;
- formative evidence used to change teaching;
- independent summative evidence;
- delayed retrieval;
- unfamiliar transfer;
- authentic creation or performance; and
- portfolio evidence with version and support history.

### Reliability and review

For consequential judgments, Starbound must use clear scoring guides, scorer
training or calibration, anchor examples, moderation of borderline work, an
appeal or resubmission process, and periodic review of whether the assessment
functions as intended.

### Open challenge, acceleration, and credit by demonstrated mastery

The Boss Door principle applies academically: a learner may attempt an advanced
assessment without completing the recommended instructional sequence first.
Seat time, platform level, age, or prior lesson completion must not be mistaken
for the outcome itself.

Credit is awarded only within the assessment's defensible evidence boundary:

- the assessment blueprint identifies the outcomes actually sampled;
- the performance conditions and permitted supports are recorded;
- the rubric requires the same standard expected after ordinary instruction;
- successful performance receives the same mastery recognition regardless of
  how the learner acquired the knowledge;
- prerequisite outcomes are waived only when they are directly sampled or
  logically inseparable from the demonstrated performance;
- missing evidence is gathered through targeted confirmation, not a demand to
  repeat an entire course;
- delayed retrieval or a second form may be required for durable-mastery or
  official-credit decisions; and
- unsuccessful attempts do not lower a grade, mastery estimate, or future
  opportunity.

At the K–12 level, this supports acceleration, compacting, and uneven learner
profiles. At the Associate of Arts stage, any official credit by examination,
prior learning, placement, residency, or transfer decision must follow the
policies of the authorized institution and applicable accreditor. Starbound may
preserve excellent evidence, but it cannot unilaterally create transcripted
college credit before that authority exists.

## 8. Records and transcript integrity

The local-first family record is a useful prototype but is not sufficient for
an accredited institution.

An institutional system would need:

- authoritative student identity and enrollment records;
- course catalog and version in force at enrollment;
- attendance or academic-engagement records where required;
- assessment and final-grade provenance;
- credits attempted and earned;
- transfer and prior-learning decisions;
- transcript controls and correction history;
- retention schedules;
- access, amendment, privacy, and release procedures;
- disaster recovery and continuity plans;
- separation of child-facing points from official academic records; and
- documented closure/teach-out and record-custodian procedures.

The family portfolio may display the same underlying evidence, but an official
record must be protected from casual edits and include an audit trail.

## 9. Student and family support

Accreditation-quality education includes the conditions that make learning
possible.

The mature system must document:

- orientation for learners and parents;
- academic advising and pathway planning;
- accessibility and accommodation processes;
- intervention and tutoring;
- library and research support;
- technology requirements and help;
- wellbeing, safety, and referral procedures;
- grievance, appeal, and complaint processes;
- academic-integrity education;
- transition guidance for schools, college, and work; and
- clear tuition, refund, privacy, and consumer-information policies if the
  program becomes commercial.

At K–2, the primary “student service” is often excellent parent guidance:
what to say, what not to reveal, when to stop, how to record an observation,
and how to distinguish productive struggle from overload.

## 10. Technology, distance learning, and AI

### Technology quality

- equivalent outcomes across supported delivery modes;
- accessible alternatives when WebGL, audio, color, motion, or a pointing
  device cannot be used;
- reliable backups and version recovery;
- security testing and least-privilege access;
- documented third-party dependencies and exit plans;
- media captions, transcripts, and source records; and
- a tested low-bandwidth or offline strategy where feasible.

### AI boundaries

AI may support recommendations, feedback drafts, content operations, and
learner practice only inside an auditable human-designed system.

AI must not silently:

- award official mastery or college credit;
- create unreviewed required curriculum;
- lower expectations based on inferred identity or permanent ability labels;
- fabricate sources or evidence;
- impersonate qualified faculty review;
- make high-stakes accommodation or disciplinary decisions; or
- reuse child data for unrelated training or advertising.

Any adaptive decision that affects instruction must retain the evidence,
model/rule version, explanation, uncertainty, override, and outcome.

### Community and external content

Future community sharing must remain asynchronous, bounded, parent-controlled,
moderated, and free of advertising and engagement optimization. Participation
cannot be required when the academic outcome can be demonstrated privately.

Community-created lessons, assessments, and worlds are external content until
formally approved. Installation count, ratings, novelty, or creator reputation
cannot substitute for subject review, assessment validity, accessibility,
source and license verification, privacy/security review, child-safety review,
version control, and academic governance. A materially remixed approved lesson
returns to review.

## 11. Fun and rigor are joint requirements

Accreditation readiness does not require removing the game world. It requires
showing that the game produces and preserves serious learning.

For every major game mechanic, the course dossier must identify:

| Design question | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| What academic relationship is the mechanic expressing? | Named concept or practice |
| What does a successful action demonstrate? | Observable evidence claim |
| What can produce a false positive? | Guessing, motor skill, interface cue, prior exposure, or help |
| How is support recorded and faded? | Defined scaffold states |
| Where is independent performance? | Named assessment moment |
| Where is later retention tested? | Retrieval schedule |
| Where is unfamiliar transfer? | New representation, situation, or creation |
| What does the child voluntarily explore afterward? | Meaningful free play connected to the concept |

Anime-style attachment, Bill Nye-style curiosity, Nintendo-style intuitive
interaction, and developmental pedagogy become academically defensible when
they improve orientation, modelling, practice, feedback, persistence,
explanation, and transfer. They are not decoration added after the lesson.

## 12. Review gates

### Gate A — Concept approval

- need and audience established;
- standards and prerequisites identified;
- construct and evidence defined;
- story/game mechanic expresses the construct;
- sources and legal constraints checked.

### Gate B — Expert review

- subject accuracy;
- developmental appropriateness;
- pedagogy and progression;
- assessment validity;
- accessibility;
- cultural and linguistic review;
- privacy and safety.

### Gate C — Pilot readiness

- working lesson and fallback;
- parent/teacher guide;
- error and support logging;
- consent and data boundaries;
- observation protocol;
- stopping criteria for frustration or fatigue.

### Gate D — Evidence review

- learning and transfer evidence;
- engagement and voluntary-play evidence;
- confusion and misconception analysis;
- accessibility failures;
- technical failures;
- documented revisions.

### Gate E — Release

- final approvals;
- version and effective date;
- archived source package;
- change log;
- monitoring and review date.

College-stage courses add faculty approval, workload/credit justification,
library sufficiency, identity/authorship procedures, student-support review,
and transfer-pathway review.

Sensitive learner work and personnel records must not be committed to the
ordinary source repository. The repository should store schemas, templates,
redacted examples, policies, and review history; protected records require an
appropriate secured system.

## 14. Near-term implementation

Before building the first full K–2 lesson, Starbound should create:

1. a course/unit dossier template;
2. an outcome-to-assessment map template;
3. a standards crosswalk schema;
4. a lesson evidence and support taxonomy;
5. an expert-review form;
6. an accessibility review checklist;
7. a source/license record;
8. a pilot observation protocol; and
9. a revision decision log.

Then the K–2 phonics unit becomes the first accreditation-style evidence case:
not merely a polished lesson, but a traceable demonstration of design,
alignment, instruction, assessment, learner evidence, analysis, and revision.
