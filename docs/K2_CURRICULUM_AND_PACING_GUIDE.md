**Version:** 0.1 — detailed curriculum architecture  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Learners in the first implementation:** Robby, age 6, K–Grade 1 foundations;
Tommy, age 7, Grade 2 with Grades 3–5 stretch opportunities  
**Planning year:** 36 instructional weeks, adaptable to a year-round calendar  
**Status:** Design and accreditation-readiness draft; not yet externally reviewed

> Build the foundations slowly enough to become automatic, the ideas deeply
> enough to transfer, and the world joyfully enough that children return by
> choice.

This document turns the
[K–12 and Associate of Arts Education Plan](K14_EDUCATION_PLAN.md) into a
specific K–2 program. It defines content, sequence, pacing, instructional
methods, assessment evidence, optional textbooks, and supplemental media.

The companion

It is not yet a lesson script. Each unit named here will receive a separate

[Accreditation and Academic Quality Framework](ACCREDITATION_AND_ACADEMIC_QUALITY_FRAMEWORK.md).

## 1. Program promises

By the end of the K–2 sequence, each learner should have:

- a secure and increasingly automatic English reading code;
- strong oral language, vocabulary, background knowledge, and love of stories;
- control of handwriting, spelling, sentence construction, and early
  composition;
- a relational understanding of quantity, number, equality, operations,
  place value, measurement, geometry, and data;
- experience predicting, investigating, modelling, explaining, and revising in
  science;
- an initial chronological, geographic, civic, economic, and cultural map of
  the human world;
- a usable foundation in everyday Spanish;
- the ability to hear, perform, represent, transform, and compose music;
- experience with algorithms, creative computing, making, and debugging;
- habits of movement, practical competence, reflection, and care;
- a dated portfolio containing independent work, revisions, and creations; and
- confidence that any Boss Door may be attempted at any time.

Florida standards are the complete compliance floor. The quality target also
draws on strong state frameworks, international curricula, disciplinary
frameworks, and learning research. Standards are crosswalked after the
curriculum's coherent conceptual sequence is established; they do not become a
random list of daily activities.

## 2. Learner placement and movement

### Grade is an administrative home, not a ceiling

Every learner has a grade-level reporting home and a separate instructional
location for each skill. A child may work at different levels in decoding,
listening comprehension, composition, number relationships, calculation,
spatial reasoning, science, music, or Spanish.

### Initial evidence conference

The first two weeks gather low-pressure evidence through games, conversation,
reading, writing, mathematics, music, and investigation. It is not one long
test.

Record:

- phonological and phonemic awareness;
- known sound-spellings and decoding/encoding patterns;
- oral reading accuracy, phrasing, and comprehension where appropriate;
- listening comprehension and oral vocabulary beyond decoding level;
- handwriting, spelling, sentence, and composition samples;
- counting, comparison, subitizing, quantity construction, place value,
  operation, measurement, pattern, and spatial reasoning;
- scientific observation, prediction, evidence language, and causal ideas;
- Spanish comprehension and spontaneous language;
- beat, rhythm, pitch, notation, and musical creation;
- interests, sensory/access needs, frustration signals, and preferred response
  modes; and
- willingness to attempt an unfamiliar boss.

Use validated tools only for their intended purpose. For example, UFLI's
intervention placement test is for selecting an intervention starting point,
not a universal screener or general growth measure.

### The Boss Door

All major unit and year-end challenges remain visible and attemptable. A valid
pass earns credit for directly demonstrated outcomes and may compact earlier
work. Missing evidence is gathered through targeted confirmation challenges,
not forced repetition of an entire sequence.

## 3. Calendar architecture

### Four nine-week arcs

| Arc | Weeks | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I — Launch | 1–8 | Establish routines, models, essential relationships, and the first world problem |
| I — Festival | 9 | Retrieve, complete boss attempts, revise, exhibit, and adjust pacing |
| II — Expansion | 10–17 | Add representations, complexity, knowledge, and independence |
| II — Festival | 18 | Retrieve across both arcs, perform, curate portfolio, and conference |
| III — Transformation | 19–26 | Apply foundations in new systems, longer texts, and multi-step work |
| III — Festival | 27 | Cumulative boss, creation, delayed transfer, and pacing decision |
| IV — Constellation | 28–35 | Integrate, create, explain, and prepare the year's public/private exhibition |
| First Star Festival | 36 | Demonstrations, performances, portfolio defense, reflection, and next-year map |

Festival weeks are not vacations from learning or weeks of standardized tests.
They contain spaced retrieval, targeted repair, optional advanced bosses,
creative synthesis, and family-visible evidence.

### Flexible year-round version

Each nine-week arc can become two four-week modules plus a one-week festival,
separated by family breaks. If illness or travel interrupts an arc, preserve
the conceptual order and reduce optional volume before dropping core practice.

| Knowledge read-aloud, literature, discussion | 5 × 20–30 min | 5 × 25–35 min | 5 × 30–45 min |
| Writing/handwriting | 5 × 15–25 min | 5 × 25–35 min | 5 × 30–45 min |
| Mathematics | 5 × 30–40 min | 5 × 40–50 min | 5 × 45–60 min |
| Science/engineering | 3 × 25–40 min | 3 × 35–50 min | 3 × 40–60 min |
| History/geography/civics | 2 × 25–35 min | 2–3 × 30–45 min | 3 × 35–50 min |
| Spanish | 4–5 × 10–20 min | 4–5 × 15–20 min | 4–5 × 15–25 min |
| Music | 3 × 20–30 min plus free lab | 3 × 25–35 min plus free lab | 3 × 30–40 min plus free lab |

| Movement/outdoors | Daily, at least 60 min total | Daily, at least 60 min total | Daily, at least 60 min total |

These are instructional ranges, not a demand for continuous seat work.
Read-alouds may happen on a couch; measurement may happen in the kitchen;
science may happen outside; music may continue voluntarily for an hour.

### Screen posture

The platform coordinates learning but does not monopolize it.

- Use the screen for simulation, animation, audio, adaptive interaction,
  immediate musical feedback, portfolio capture, and inaccessible phenomena.
- Use books, paper, manipulatives, household materials, instruments, outdoor
  observation, conversation, movement, and construction whenever they are the
  stronger medium.
- A typical K–2 day should not require more than roughly 60–90 minutes of
  essential platform interaction, split into short sessions. This is a design
  target, not a medical rule or a reason to interrupt meaningful creation.
- Every core outcome must have an accessible non-WebGL path.

### Sample day

1. **Morning launch:** oral retrieval, read-aloud, calendar/weather observation.
2. **Code workshop:** phonemic awareness, phonics, decoding, spelling,
   handwriting.
3. **Movement break.**
4. **Mathematics mission:** orienting problem, material model, explanation,
   practice, boss or creation.
5. **Outside/lunch/practical life.**
6. **Investigation block:** science or history inquiry with reading/writing.
7. **Spanish micro-episode.**
8. **Music/art/computing studio.**
9. **Two-minute reflection:** what changed, what helped, what to try tomorrow.

## 5. Lesson and unit anatomy

### A lesson contains three to five learn-and-practice sections

For younger learners, the full episode loop may span several days. A typical
lesson cluster contains:

1. **Remember and orient** — retrieve something known and meet a meaningful
   problem.
2. **Act and model** — manipulate the idea and make its relationship visible.
3. **Name and practice** — add precise language and several successful varied
   repetitions.
4. **Explain and fade** — verbalize the relationship while one support is
   removed.
5. **Boss and create** — perform independently, transfer, or make something.

Every unit contains:

- a compelling question or story problem;
- a named governing relationship;
- prerequisite and misconception map;
- cumulative practice;
- an optional read-along textbook chapter;
- at least one real-world or offline investigation;
- Normal, Hard, and Master variants;
- an always-open boss;
- a Window Upward;
- delayed retrieval; and
- dated portfolio evidence.

### Spacing and retrieval rhythm

| Time after initial learning | Expected return |
| --- | --- |
| Same lesson | Immediate guided and independent application |
| Next session | Brief retrieval before new material |
| 3–7 days | Interleaved item or changed representation |
| 2–4 weeks | Cumulative mission or application |
| Festival week | Boss, explanation, or creation using the idea |
| Later arc | Unfamiliar transfer and durable-mastery evidence |

Retrieval is brief and purposeful. It does not become constant testing or a
punitive daily streak.

## 6. Mastery, evidence, and reporting

### Four evidence states

1. **Exposed** — encountered or explored.
2. **Supported** — succeeded with recorded prompts, models, TTS, choices, or
   adult help.
3. **Independent** — demonstrated the defined outcome under valid conditions.
4. **Durable transfer** — retrieved later and used in a changed context.

### Minimum unit evidence

- one independent performance;
- one explanation or representation;
- one delayed retrieval;
- one creation, investigation, or unfamiliar application;
- support and difficulty record;
- dated artifact where the outcome produces one; and
- parent observation when the important action occurs off-platform.

### TTS validity

Natural TTS is an access and comprehension tool, not a secret answer key.

- Read-aloud is freely available for stories, directions, science, history,
  Spanish support, and content above decoding level.
- During independent decoding evidence, the target word or sentence cannot be
  spoken before the learner attempts it.
- After the attempt, audio may model pronunciation and support correction.
- TTS use is recorded only when it changes what the assessment can claim.
- Listening comprehension and decoding remain separate evidence.

### Festivals and portfolio conferences

At weeks 9, 18, 27, and 36, the child:

- chooses work to display;
- revises at least one earlier artifact;
- attempts one recommended or self-selected boss;
- explains one idea to a family member or recording;
- performs or shares music/Spanish where comfortable;
- reflects on help and strategy; and
- helps choose a goal for the next arc.

## 7. English language arts architecture

K–2 language arts runs in three coordinated strands:

1. **Foundational code:** phonemic awareness, phonics, decoding, encoding,
   handwriting, fluency, and morphology.
2. **Language and knowledge:** rich read-alouds, vocabulary, literature,
   science, history, art, discussion, and comprehension.
3. **Composition:** oral rehearsal, handwriting, spelling, sentence work,
   narrative, information, opinion/argument, explanation, and revision.

The strands meet often but are not confused. A child may discuss a complex
story far above the text they can independently decode.

### 7.1 Foundational reading progression

Use one internally coherent phonics sequence. UFLI Foundations is the primary
implementation reference; Starbound activities and stories remain original.
Do not mix the lesson order of multiple phonics programs casually.

| Phase | Concepts | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Speech and print | Words, syllables, onset-rime, phoneme identity, blend/segment orally, print direction, letter formation | Move one token per sound; identify and form taught letters |
| B — First code | High-utility consonants; short vowels; continuous blending; CVC reading/spelling; a small set of explicitly taught irregular words | Read and spell unfamiliar regular CVC words and controlled sentences |
| C — Adjacent consonants | Digraphs, common blends, final spelling patterns, nasalized vowel patterns, plural/verb endings where decodable | Transform and explain word chains; read connected decodable text |
| D — Long-vowel systems | Silent-e patterns and common vowel teams; compare spellings for the same sound | Sort by sound and spelling; decode/encode in text |
| E — Complex code | R-controlled vowels, diphthongs, variant vowels, soft sounds, silent letters, contractions | Select among plausible spellings; read increasingly authentic text |
| F — Word structure | Inflectional endings, compound words, syllable types/division, prefixes, suffixes, base words, early roots | Read and build multisyllable/morphologically complex words |

Advancement depends on evidence, not birthday. A phase may overlap the next
when new instruction begins while automaticity continues developing.

### Kindergarten foundational pacing

| Weeks | Core focus | Boss evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Oral blend/segment; concept of word; print direction; first consonant sounds and formations | Segment a 2–3 phoneme word and map taught sounds |
| 5–8 | Short `a`; continuous blending; first CVC word chains; handwriting | Read and spell unfamiliar short-`a` CVC words |
| 9 | Retrieval festival; optional later-vowel boss | Independent short controlled sentence |
| 10–13 | Short `i`; contrast `/ă/` and `/ĭ/`; add high-utility consonants | Discriminate, read, and spell mixed short-`a/i` words |
| 14–17 | Short `o`; cumulative CVC reading; first decodable micro-stories | Read a new cumulative micro-story and answer orally |
| 18 | Festival and repair | Decode without picture guessing |
| 19–22 | Short `e`; phoneme substitution; sentence-level spelling | Transform one phoneme at a time and explain the change |
| 23–26 | Short `u`; all short vowels; common digraph introduction if ready | Read/spell mixed short-vowel words |
| 27 | Cumulative boss | Controlled passage plus dictated sentence |
| 28–31 | Digraphs and common final patterns; adjacent consonant exposure | Read a new word with a taught digraph |
| 32–35 | Consolidation; early blends for ready learners; fluency through rereading | Accurate first read and smoother purposeful reread |
| 36 | First Star Festival | Publish and read a decodable creature field guide |

short confirmation bosses, targeted repair when a genuine gap appears, and
more time for morphology, fluency, vocabulary, interpretation, and writing.

### Comprehension progression

| K | Grade 1 | Grade 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retell beginning/middle/end; identify character, setting, problem; answer/ask questions; predict from evidence; compare two texts orally | Describe character response and lesson; sequence and cause; use text details; compare accounts; identify main topic/key details; explain word meaning | Infer with named evidence; analyze character perspective and structure; compare versions/sources; identify main idea and support; explain cause, viewpoint, and author's choices |

Tommy's Hard and Master texts may use Grades 3–5 sentence structure, vocabulary,

Support protects access without flattening the thinking.

### 7.3 Writing and handwriting

| Arc | K | Grade 1 | Grade 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Grip/posture/access alternative; letter formation; draw-label-dictate; chronological oral narrative | Legible formation; complete sentences; capitals/end marks; sequence two or more events | Sentence expansion/combining; paragraph focus; narrative pace and logical sequence |
| II | Encode taught sounds; caption; oral rehearsal; who/doing/where | Narrative details and closure; informative sentence set; spelling from phonics | Narrative with transitions/ending; informative paragraph with facts and definitions |
| III | Two connected ideas; simple information page; revise by adding | Opinion with reason; question/answer; revise word choice and clarity | Opinion/argument with reasons; explanation of a process; revise organization and evidence |
| IV | Short class/individual book; reread and improve | Multi-sentence publication across genres; basic keyboarding | Multi-paragraph stretch where ready; research from provided sources; edit and publish |

Writing movement:

> Manipulate → rehearse aloud → write/dictate → read back → revise one
> meaningful feature → publish for a real or imagined audience.

Grammar and conventions are taught in service of clear sentences and revisited
in authentic writing. Handwriting and keyboarding are both developed; neither
is allowed to prevent a child from demonstrating ideas when it is not the
construct being assessed.

## 8. Mathematics architecture

Starbound mathematics combines:

- Florida B.E.S.T. grade-level outcomes and Mathematical Thinking and
  Reasoning;
- the Singapore focus on mathematical problem solving, concepts, skills,
  processes, metacognition, and attitudes;
- Davydov-inspired comparison, measurement, equality, general relationships,
  and movement from general structure to cases;
- Galperin's material → model → speech → mental action progression;
- coherent problem and representation banks from properly licensed elementary
  mathematics resources; and
- enough deliberate practice for reliable calculation and fact retrieval.

### Governing mathematical relationships

These recur every year:

- same/different and attribute;
- greater/less/equal;
- whole, parts, and composition;
- change and inverse change;
- difference and comparison;
- unit iteration and measurement;
- position, shape, transformation, and invariance;
- pattern and generalization;
- equality as a relationship, not an “answer comes next” signal;
- representation across object, diagram, language, and symbol; and
- data as evidence about a question.

### Mathematics practice policy

- Fluency is accurate, reasonably efficient, flexible, and based on understood
  relationships.
- Timed work may be used privately to observe automaticity after competence,
  never as public pressure or the main mathematics identity.
- Practice mixes worked examples, completion problems, construction, error
  analysis, explanation, retrieval, and non-routine transfer.
- Concrete materials are faded when the learner can coordinate the
  relationship mentally; they remain available as an access tool.
- Hard and Master deepen construction, representation, explanation, and
  transfer. They do not merely increase numbers or reduce time.

### Mathematics supplements

- **Florida:** official B.E.S.T. Mathematics standards and current K–2
  instructional guides.
- **International reference:** Singapore Primary Mathematics syllabus and its
  problem-solving framework.
- **OER problem/reference bank:** first-edition IM K–5 Math materials under CC
  BY 4.0 where fit and attribution permit. IM v.360 is CC BY-NC 4.0 and must be
  isolated if used in a noncommercial context.
- **Starbound core:** original *Relations Before Rules* read-along chapters,
  interactive models, story systems, practice, and boss assessments.

## 9. Science and engineering architecture

Science is organized around phenomena and model revision, not vocabulary
slides followed by a quiz.

The recurring movement is:

> Notice → wonder → predict → investigate → represent data → build/revise a
> model → explain → apply or design.

### Practice progression

| K | Grade 1 | Grade 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe with senses/tools; compare; predict; draw what happened; identify a pattern; say “I think… because…” | Ask testable questions with help; change one thing; measure; organize observations; compare evidence to prediction; revise a model | Plan a fair comparison with support; measure repeatedly; display data; distinguish observation/inference; use a model for cause; argue from evidence |

### Kindergarten science pacing

| Arc | Phenomenon and core concepts | Signature investigation/design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Forces and motion:** pushes/pulls, strength/direction, contact, collision, position, motion change | Move a stranded snack capsule through a course without touching it directly at the end |
| II | **Weather and sunlight:** daily patterns, warming surfaces, shade, weather observation, preparation | Compare surfaces in sun/shade and design shelter |
| III | **Needs and environments:** plant/animal needs, habitats, human use of resources, care and impact | Observe local life and model how needs connect to place |
| IV | **Senses, signals, and materials:** senses gather information; material attributes; tools extend observation | Design a mystery-identification station with safe sensory evidence |

### Grade 1 science pacing

| Arc | Phenomenon and core concepts | Signature investigation/design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Light:** sources, illumination, transparent/translucent/opaque, reflection, path model | Make a dark reading shelter work and explain why |
| II | **Sound:** vibration, pitch/volume patterns, communication, material effects | Build and revise a signal instrument |
| III | **Plants, animals, and survival:** structures, needs, parent/offspring patterns, behavior and information | Design a habitat support from evidence without assuming all organisms need people |
| IV | **Sun, moon, stars, and simple machines:** observable sky patterns; lever/wheel/pulley/inclined-plane experiences | Keep a sky-pattern log and build a simple rescue machine |

### Grade 2 science pacing

| Arc | Phenomenon and core concepts | Signature investigation/design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Matter and toy design:** observable properties, solids/liquids, suitability, reversible/irreversible change window | Select and test materials for a new toy |
| II | **Land and water change:** wind/water effects, slow/fast change, maps/models, design to reduce impact | Build, test, and revise an erosion solution |
| III | **Habitats and biodiversity:** organisms in places, needs, relationships, comparison across habitats | Conduct a local biodiversity survey and habitat model |
| IV | **Electricity and magnetism:** circuits as systems, conductors/insulators at an introductory level, magnetic attraction/repulsion, poles, patterns, Earth-field Window Upward | Build a magnetic or simple-circuit rescue device and explain its limits |

### Shared family science labs

Robby and Tommy may share the phenomenon while producing different evidence.
For example, in the magnet lab:

- Robby sorts objects by observed interaction, predicts, tests, and describes
  attraction without assuming all metal is magnetic.
- Tommy maps pole interactions, designs a fair test for distance/strength,
  records data, and explores an optional field/Earth-magnetism model.

### Science texts and video supplements

- **Primary OER unit reference:** OpenSciEd Elementary's four units per grade,
  scope/sequence, and teacher handbook, adapted only under applicable terms.
- **Knowledge text reference:** free Core Knowledge Science K–2 units.
- **Video/article library:** NASA Science for Kids and age-appropriate federal,
  museum, university, and public-media sources selected per lesson.
- **Video rule:** preview, record source/date/rights, use a specific short segment
  for a named objective, provide captions/transcript, ask for prediction before
  the reveal when appropriate, and provide a no-video alternative.
- **Starbound core:** original *Young Scientist's Field Manual*, household labs,
  simulations, models, and boss investigations.

No lesson depends on a third-party video remaining online. Videos supply
phenomena, expertise, or otherwise inaccessible observations; they do not

## 10. History, geography, civics, and economics

Young children receive real content through maps, timelines, stories,
artifacts, photographs, oral history, biography, and age-appropriate primary
sources. Inquiry does not mean asking children to reason without background
knowledge.

### Recurring inquiry arc

1. Ask a compelling, concrete question.
2. Build essential background knowledge.
3. Observe a map, object, photograph, story, or source.
4. Distinguish what is seen/heard from what is inferred.
5. Compare another source or perspective.
6. Make a supported claim or representation.
7. Communicate or take a small informed action.

### Year progression

| Arc | K | Grade 1 | Grade 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Self, family, roles, needs, personal timeline | Family/community change; rules and reasons; map of familiar places | Local community systems; sources and evidence; map scale/direction seeds |
| II | Home, school, neighborhood, maps and symbols | Continents/oceans, cultures and stories, needs/wants/work | Early world civilizations selected for breadth and connection; geography shapes life |
| III | Then/now, artifacts, helpers, fairness | Exploration, movement, encounter, multiple viewpoints; early American communities | Migration/immigration and Indigenous continuity; trade, technology, and change |
| IV | Celebrations/traditions with within-culture variety; care for community | Colonies-to-independence introduction; civic participation | Government levels and services; rights/responsibilities; money, choice, scarcity, public problem |

### Required cautions

- Do not present cultures as costumes, foods, or a single fixed “way people
  live.”
- Distinguish myth, traditional story, historical claim, and evidence.
- Include Indigenous people in past and present.
- Use age-appropriate truth rather than patriotic mythology or cynical
  simplification.
- Avoid simulations that ask children to role-play enslavement, oppression, or
  trauma.
- Present disagreement and multiple perspectives without implying that every
  claim has equal evidence.

### Supplements

- Core Knowledge History and Geography K–2 as a free content-rich reference,
  reviewed and broadened where needed.
- Library of Congress photographs, maps, recordings, and Observe–Reflect–Question
  tools for carefully selected primary sources.
- C3 Framework Inquiry Arc for disciplinary practice.
- Local museums, libraries, parks, family oral histories, maps, and civic
  institutions.
- Original Starbound *Living Atlas* read-along chapters and source mysteries.

## 11. Spanish

Robby and Tommy share the same communicative world. Tommy may read, combine,
and extend more language, but neither child is rushed through a vocabulary dump.

### Instructional principles

- high-frequency, meaningful language in small cumulative sets;
- abundant comprehensible listening from proficient human speakers;
- gesture, image, object, action, story, and context before translation lists;
- one communicative purpose at a time;
- listen → choose/act → repeat with meaning → recombine → interact → present;
- brief frequent practice rather than one long weekly session;
- pronunciation modelled naturally without shaming accent;
- culture connected to real people, places, practices, and variation; and
- interpretive, interpersonal, and presentational evidence over time.

### 36-week spiral

| Weeks | Communicative mission | Core language concepts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Meet someone | `hola`, `buenos días`, `adiós`, names, `¿Cómo estás?`, feelings, polite response |
| 5–8 | Identify self and others | `yo`, `tú`, `soy`, `eres`, `me llamo`, child/person, basic descriptors |
| 9 | Plaza festival | Greeting/identity boss with an unfamiliar speaker clip |
| 10–13 | Family and belonging | mamá, papá, hermano/a, family names, `mi/tu`, `es`, `tengo` |
| 14–17 | Hunger, thirst, and food | `tengo hambre/sed`, foods, `quiero`, `por favor`, `gracias`, yes/no |
| 18 | Café festival | Order, respond, and repair misunderstanding |
| 19–22 | Likes and dislikes | `me gusta/no me gusta`, food/play/music, ask another person's preference |
| 23–26 | Body, needs, and simple actions | body words, `me duele` as appropriate, `necesito`, come/go/eat/drink/play commands in context |
| 27 | Rescue festival | Follow and give familiar directions in a comic scenario |
| 28–31 | Home, school, and location | common objects/places, `aquí/allí`, `hay`, `¿dónde?`, simple location phrases |
| 32–35 | Daily mini-stories | combine identity, family, needs, likes, place, and action; sequence first/then at stretch level |
| 36 | Plaza del Sol exhibition | Short conversation, song/story, and self-selected boss |

### Differentiation

| Robby | Tommy |
| --- | --- |
| Demonstrate comprehension through action/picture; use words and memorized phrases; repeat and recombine orally | Interpret short authentic/learner texts; ask/answer with simple sentences; read/write familiar phrases; add reasons or sequence where ready |

The aim is consistent Novice-range growth, not a false promise of fluency from
app completion. ACTFL Can-Do statements describe performance across repeated
contexts and are not a one-time checklist.

### Audio and video supplements

- Commission or license human-speaker clips for the Starbound core dialogue.
- Use GPB SALSA episodes selectively as optional comprehensible-input stories;
  each episode receives a before/during/after task rather than passive viewing.
- Use music, stories, and short media from varied Spanish-speaking communities
  with rights, transcript, country/region, and cultural context recorded.
- TTS may support drafts and accessibility but does not replace the primary
  human pronunciation model.

**Optional read-along textbook:** *Mi Mundo: Spanish for Everyday Adventures* —
one illustrated micro-chapter per communicative mission, with tap-to-hear human

## 12. Music

Music remains the same core laboratory for both boys. Difficulty changes the
number of layers, notation demand, independence, and transfer—not access to the
creative tool.

### Learning movement

> Hear → move → imitate → represent → read → transform → compose → perform →
> explain.

### 36-week spiral

| Weeks | Core concepts | Interactive lab and boss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Sound/silence; steady beat; long/short; voice/instrument/environment | Move to beat, place sound tiles, create and save a four-beat pattern |
| 5–8 | Quarter-note/rest concepts; repeated patterns; color-linked note changes | Drag notation, hear immediate result, repair a broken loop |
| 9 | Rhythm Grove festival | Perform and modify a saved beat |
| 10–13 | Pitch high/low; melodic direction/contour; same/different | Move colored notes on a spatial staff and sing/play the contour |
| 14–17 | Phrase, question/answer, repetition/contrast; A/B form | Compose two phrases and choose a form |
| 18 | Listening festival | Identify and create contrast through pitch/rhythm/form |
| 19–22 | Tempo, dynamics, articulation, timbre; expressive intent | Score a comic chase, nap, meal, or awkward meeting |
| 23–26 | Staff orientation; steps/skips/repeats; note names introduced through used patterns | Staff-hop and notation boss; Robby may use relative/color notation, Tommy adds names |
| 27 | Performance festival | Read, perform, alter, and explain an unfamiliar short pattern |
| 28–31 | Layering, ostinato, accompaniment, harmony seed; loop length | Build two compatible loop layers; test consonance/tension as an experience |
| 32–35 | Composition cycle: plan, draft, listen, revise, title, notate, perform | Create a 16–32 beat piece with intentional form and expression |
| 36 | Rhythm Grove concert | Save to library, perform/share privately, and explain one choice |

### Difficulty

- **Normal:** color plus shape/position; audible preview; one layer; visible beat
  grid; guided choices.
- **Hard:** reduced color reliance; rests and phrase construction; two layers;
  describe a choice.
- **Master:** unfamiliar meter/polyrhythm or harmony window; staff notation;
  variation; independent form and performance.

Color never carries meaning alone. Shape, vertical position, label, sound, and
keyboard access provide redundant cues.

### Supplements

- Carnegie Hall Music Educators Toolbox K–2 lessons, videos, activities, and
  assessments for rhythm/meter, form, expression, pitch, singing, and
  instrumental work.
- Smithsonian Folkways lessons and recordings when terms permit.
- Public-domain and appropriately licensed music from varied traditions.
- Chrome Music Lab as an optional external experimentation link, not the
  required core or record system.
- Original Starbound *Color Music Lab*, human-recorded instruments, notation
  engine, loop library, and composition portfolio.

## 16. Video and media policy

### A video enters a lesson only when it does one of these

- shows a phenomenon that is dangerous, remote, microscopic, enormous, slow,
  fast, historical, or otherwise difficult to observe;
- supplies a human pronunciation, performance, expert, place, artifact, or
  perspective that the platform cannot authentically generate;
- models a disciplinary practice; or
- gives a concise knowledge-rich explanation after the learner has a reason to
  seek it.

### Required media record

- title, provider, direct URL, creator, publication/update date, access date;
- duration and exact segment used;
- learning objective and before/during/after prompt;
- age fit and content notes;
- captions, transcript, language, and accessibility;
- license/terms and whether use is link, embed, quotation, or adaptation;
- advertising/tracking/account requirements;
- parent-facing preview;
- fallback text/image/experiment; and
- review date and link-health status.

### Initial trusted source shelf

- NASA Science for Kids and NASA learning resources;
- Smithsonian museums and Smithsonian Learning Lab/Folkways;
- Library of Congress primary sources;
- Carnegie Hall Music Educators Toolbox;
- GPB SALSA;
- OpenSciEd media inside applicable unit terms;
- public universities, national laboratories, NOAA, USGS, National Park
  Service, and other primary public institutions; and
- carefully reviewed public-media resources such as BBC materials when linking
  and territorial terms allow.

No autoplay playlists, algorithmic “next video,” child account requirement, or
unreviewed comments appear inside the lesson experience.

## 18. Pacing decisions and intervention

### Continue when

- the learner can perform the current target with decreasing support;
- errors are informative but not dominated by an untaught prerequisite;
- cumulative review remains successful enough to add one new element; and
- the child can explain or represent the governing relationship at the
  expected level.

### Pause and intensify when

- guessing replaces decoding or reasoning;
- the same prerequisite error persists across representations;
- support is not fading;
- frustration, fatigue, or avoidance obscures what the child knows;
- earlier retrieval collapses; or
- the task requires language, motor, sensory, or interface access the child has
  not been given.

Intensification changes group size, clarity, model, number of successful
repetitions, feedback, and spacing. It does not simply assign more of the same
failed worksheet.

### Compact or accelerate when

- a boss or targeted assessment directly demonstrates the outcomes;
- performance generalizes across more than one item or context;
- support conditions are valid and recorded; and
- a later retrieval confirms durability when the decision is consequential.

Compacted practice becomes optional exploration, creative application, or a
later retrieval—not mandatory busywork.
