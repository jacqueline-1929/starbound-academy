# Starbound Academy K–12 and Associate of Arts Education Plan

**Version:** 0.1 — curriculum architecture sketch  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Immediate build band:** Kindergarten through Grade 2  
**Long horizon:** Kindergarten through Grade 12 plus a two-year Associate of
Arts stage; “K–14” remains internal shorthand only

> The goal is not to rush children through school. It is to give them a long,
> coherent intellectual adventure in which knowledge, judgment, creativity,
> humor, and independence grow together.

This document defines the educational journey. The
[World, Experience, and Pedagogy Design Bible](STARBOUND_ACADEMY_DESIGN_BIBLE.md)
defines how that journey should feel and function as a game.

---

## 1. Status and limits

This is a curriculum architecture, not a claim of accreditation, a complete
course catalogue, or legal advice. It establishes:

- the graduate profile;
- the instructional method shared by all subjects;
- the K–12 and Associate of Arts stage progression;
- a provisional year-by-year intellectual spine;
- the vertical progression for each subject; and
- the order in which detailed courses should be built and validated.

Every detailed course must later receive a standards crosswalk, lesson-level
scope and sequence, source and license review, accessibility review, assessment
plan, and expert review. Florida requirements form the compliance floor for
the current family. Strong U.S. frameworks and high-performing international
systems form comparison points, not trophies to copy without context.

## 2. The Starbound graduate

At the end of the Associate of Arts stage, a Starbound learner should be able to:

1. **Read deeply.** Read literature, history, science, mathematics, philosophy,
   journalism, and practical documents with comprehension, skepticism, empathy,
   and pleasure.
2. **Write and speak clearly.** Tell stories, explain mechanisms, build an
   argument, revise honestly, discuss disagreement, and adapt communication for
   a real audience.
3. **Think mathematically.** See structures and relationships, calculate
   fluently, model uncertainty, use proof and counterexample, and know when a
   quantitative claim is misleading.
4. **Investigate nature.** Ask testable questions, design fair investigations,
   use models, analyze evidence, understand major scientific theories, and
   distinguish uncertainty from ignorance.
5. **Understand human worlds.** Reason historically and geographically,
   understand institutions and economies, compare cultures without caricature,
   and participate constructively in civic life.
6. **Create.** Compose music, make visual or dramatic work, design systems,
   write programs, and improve work through critique.
7. **Use more than one language.** Communicate meaningfully in Spanish and
   approach language as a window into people, history, and thought.
8. **Use tools without being used by them.** Understand computing, media, data,
   privacy, algorithms, and AI well enough to create responsibly and question
   outputs.
9. **Care for a life.** Practice movement, health, household competence,
   financial judgment, collaboration, emotional regulation, and stewardship of
   the natural world.
10. **Direct further learning.** Identify what they do not know, choose a
    strategy, seek help, practice, retrieve, reflect, and transfer knowledge to
    an unfamiliar problem.

The desired result is neither a test-taking machine nor a child left to
"discover" an entire civilization alone. It is a knowledgeable, capable,
curious person who can accept guidance and increasingly direct their own work.

## 3. Curriculum synthesis

Starbound is deliberately synthetic. No country or named program supplies the
whole answer.

| Source tradition | What Starbound adopts | What Starbound avoids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Florida B.E.S.T. and Florida course expectations | The local compliance floor and auditable grade-level outcomes | Treating the minimum standard as the ceiling |
| Massachusetts and New York frameworks | Strong comparison expectations for literacy, knowledge, writing, and disciplinary reading | A checklist curriculum with no coherent story |
| Singapore mathematics | Problem solving at the center; concepts, skills, processes, metacognition, and attitudes; relational understanding and big ideas | Memorized procedures without meaning |
| Soviet developmental traditions | Elkonin sound analysis; Galperin's movement from material action to mental action; Davydov's general relationships; Vygotskian guided development; supported productive difficulty | Political indoctrination, authoritarian classroom culture, or uncritical historical imitation |
| Estonia, Finland, and Norway | Learner agency, play, participation, transversal competence, interdisciplinary work, creation, reflection, and learning-to-learn | Mistaking freedom for the absence of explicit teaching |
| Core Knowledge and knowledge-rich curricula | A coherent, cumulative map of literature, history, geography, science, music, and art | Using one publisher's sequence as unquestionable canon |
| Learning science | Explicit modelling for novices, worked examples, deliberate practice, retrieval, spacing, feedback, varied examples, and transfer | Constant testing, shallow gamified recall, or optimizing only what is easy to measure |
| NGSS, C3, CSTA, ACTFL, and arts frameworks | Authentic disciplinary practices and coherent progressions | Generic "skills" detached from substantive knowledge |

### The key reconciliation

Excellent instruction is neither nonstop lecture nor minimally guided
discovery. Starbound uses explicit teaching to provide powerful tools, then
requires the learner to use those tools in investigation, explanation,
creation, and unfamiliar transfer.

For a novice:

> Clear model → supported action → explanation → varied practice → independent
> performance → creative transfer.

As expertise grows, the learner assumes more responsibility for selecting the
model, designing the investigation, judging evidence, and planning the work.

## 4. The Starbound instructional operating system

Every subject uses the same deep rhythm, adapted to its discipline.

1. **Retrieve.** Bring back a small amount of prior learning without notes.
2. **Orient.** Encounter a meaningful problem, phenomenon, text, sound, claim,
   or design need.
3. **Act materially.** Move counters, phoneme tokens, notes, vectors, evidence
   cards, code blocks, lab materials, or another inspectable object.
4. **Reveal the model.** Make the governing relationship visible in a diagram,
   timeline, equation, map, score, sentence frame, or simulation.
5. **Name and explain.** Supply precise language and ask the learner to explain
   what changes, what remains invariant, and why.
6. **Practice deliberately.** Use short, successful repetitions with feedback;
   vary surface features while protecting the central idea.
7. **Fade support.** Remove a cue, representation, worked step, or prompt.
8. **Perform independently.** Produce evidence that can support a mastery
   judgment and portfolio entry.
9. **Transfer and create.** Apply the relationship in an unfamiliar problem or
   use it to make something personally meaningful.
10. **Return later.** Schedule spaced retrieval and a future application.

This is the cross-subject expression of:

> Act it → model it → explain it → internalize it → transfer it.

### Serious knowledge, playful delivery

Humor, narrative, absurdity, surprise, and game feel are instructional design
tools. They earn attention and memory, but the concept remains the mechanic.
The child might rescue a moon dumpling from a gravitational disaster, but the
solution still requires a real model of force and motion.

### Windows Upward

Each unit may contain a brief, optional glimpse of a connected advanced idea.
These experiences are recorded as exposure, never disguised as mastery. They
create intellectual horizon without breaking the progression.

## 5. Five developmental eras

### Era I — Launch: Kindergarten–Grade 2

**Developmental task:** turn language, quantity, nature, rhythm, movement, and
social life into objects the child can deliberately notice and control.

**Instructional balance:** short explicit models, abundant material action,
playful repetition, oral language, movement, read-alouds, experiments, and
creation. The adult supplies structure while the child supplies action.

**By the end of Grade 2, learners should:**

- read increasingly complex decodable and authentic text with comprehension;
- write legible sentences and short compositions with expanding control;
- model place value, operations, equality, comparison, shape, measure, and
  early data relationships rather than merely recite arithmetic;
- make predictions, observe carefully, record evidence, and revise a simple
  model;
- locate themselves in family, community, map, timeline, and civic routines;
- hold a simple Spanish interaction;
- keep a beat, read and alter elementary notation, and compose a short loop;
- create with physical and digital tools; and
- reflect on help used, effort, errors, and the next attempt.

**Signature exhibition:** *The First Star Festival* — read or narrate an
original story, demonstrate a mathematical model, present a small
investigation, perform a composition, and greet visitors in Spanish.

### Era II — Frontier: Grades 3–5

**Developmental task:** expand from foundational tools into organized domains
of knowledge and increasingly independent practice.

**Instructional balance:** explicit knowledge building, richer independent
reading, multi-step models, guided inquiry, collaborative creation, and
scheduled retrieval across years.

**By the end of Grade 5, learners should:**

- read substantial children's literature and knowledge-rich nonfiction;
- write narrative, explanatory, and evidence-based pieces through revision;
- reason fluently with whole numbers, fractions, decimals, measurement,
  geometry, data, and early generalized relationships;
- explain systems involving matter, energy, forces, organisms, Earth, and
  space using evidence and models;
- build connected historical and geographical knowledge across world regions
  and early U.S. history;
- sustain practical Spanish conversations and comprehend short stories;
- compose, perform, program, build, and document; and
- plan a multi-week project with adult checkpoints.

**Signature exhibition:** *Worlds in Motion* — an interdisciplinary museum of
models, maps, narratives, data, music, and designed solutions.

### Era III — Guild: Grades 6–8

**Developmental task:** enter the disciplines as an apprentice and learn their
distinctive tools, standards of evidence, and forms of argument.

**Instructional balance:** strong subject instruction, labs and seminars,
worked examples followed by independent problems, longer projects, source
criticism, and explicit study strategies.

**By the end of Grade 8, learners should:**

- analyze complex literature and informational sources;
- write sustained arguments, explanations, narratives, and research products;
- complete a coherent path through pre-algebra, algebra, geometry, probability,
  and statistics foundations;
- investigate life, physical, Earth, and space science with measurement,
  modelling, and uncertainty;
- reason across ancient, medieval, early modern, modern, U.S., and global
  histories without reducing history to trivia;
- participate in civic inquiry using claims, evidence, and competing accounts;
- communicate in connected Spanish across familiar topics;
- design software and physical systems, understand data and networks, and
  practice digital ethics; and
- produce and critique art and music using disciplinary language.

**Signature exhibition:** *Guild Defense* — publicly defend a research claim,
demonstrate a technical or artistic creation, and answer questions about
evidence and design choices.

### Era IV — Constellation: Grades 9–12

**Developmental task:** gain disciplinary power, integrate perspectives, and
make responsible choices about specialization.

**Instructional balance:** rigorous sequences in the major disciplines,
seminars, laboratories, studios, independent reading, authentic audiences,
apprenticeships where possible, and increasing choice within a common core.

**By the end of Grade 12, learners should:**

- complete a broad, transcript-ready secondary education meeting Florida
  graduation expectations, with auditable course evidence;
- read foundational and contemporary works from multiple cultures and fields;
- write researched arguments and lucid explanatory prose;
- complete mathematics through at least Algebra II plus statistics and a
  suitable advanced option such as precalculus, calculus, discrete math, or
  data science;
- complete laboratory biology, chemistry, and physics/Earth-space sequences;
- understand U.S. and world history, government, economics, geography, and
  contemporary institutions;
- reach a meaningful intermediate Spanish proficiency, with an optional path
  beyond it;
- complete substantial work in computing, arts, health, and practical life;
- evaluate media, data, and AI claims; and
- execute a yearlong capstone from proposal to public defense.

**Signature exhibition:** *Constellation Capstone* — a substantial research,
engineering, civic, entrepreneurial, or artistic work with a mentor, process
log, revision trail, and public defense.

### Era V — Associate of Arts Stage: two postsecondary years

**Developmental task:** connect specialized knowledge to the enduring human
questions and methods that support university, work, citizenship, and a
meaningful life.

This is not "high school again." “Grades 13–14” is only an internal timeline
shorthand. The formal target is a 60-semester-credit Associate in Arts structure
comparable to the lower division of a baccalaureate program: 36 credits of
general education and 24 credits of transfer-oriented electives and
prerequisites. Credit, an A.A. degree, articulation, or transfer value exists
only when an authorized institution awards it and the relevant receiving
institution accepts it.

**Common core:**

- rhetoric, composition, and research;
- formal and informal logic;
- quantitative reasoning, statistics, and data ethics;
- scientific reasoning through laboratory or field investigation;
- world history, political thought, economics, and civic institutions;
- literature, philosophy, religion/worldviews, and the arts across cultures;
- advanced language or intercultural study;
- computing, media, and AI literacy;
- practical finance, health, collaboration, and project management; and
- a two-semester thesis, performance, venture, design, or investigation.

**Signature exhibition:** *The Starbound Defense* — submit a publishable
portfolio and defend a major question or creation before a mixed panel.

## 5.1 Four experience worlds across five instructional eras

The five instructional eras above are grouped into four major experience
worlds. Academic bands and visual worlds solve different problems: the
instructional eras protect developmental and curricular precision, while the
experience worlds provide the feeling of entering a new age of a continuous
adventure.

| Experience world | Instructional eras inside it | Presentation progression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Wonder Archipelago, K–5** | Launch Years, K–2; Frontier Years, 3–5 | Begins with large narrated islands and direct manipulation, then expands into richer maps, longer projects, connected systems, and independent planning |
| **The Guild Realms, 6–8** | Guild Years | Discipline-based guilds, expedition zones, laboratories, archives, workshops, strategy, investigation, and defended work |
| **The Constellation Network, 9–12** | Constellation Years | Course-based campuses and stations, mature simulations, research notebooks, seminars, labs, specialization, and capstones |
| **The Great Conversation, A.A.** | Associate of Arts Stage | Collegiate seminars, syllabi, credit maps, archives, labs, studios, fieldwork, thesis development, and public defense |

Every experience world must receive original art direction, terrain or spatial
logic, sound, interface density, game families, and narrative stakes suited to
its learners. It must not feel like the K–5 map with darker colors and smaller
type. At the same time, shared interaction grammar, accessibility, records,
avatar identity, points, creations, Learning Memories, and portfolio continuity
prevent the transitions from becoming four unrelated products.

A learner has a current **academic home world**, not a prison. Previous worlds
remain open for review, creation, mentoring, and nostalgia. Future-world boss
doors and challenge previews may be attempted at any time. Success can create
valid evidence when the assessment conditions are met, while enrollment,
course-credit, and degree claims remain governed by the formal requirements of
the relevant program.

The spine gives each year a center of gravity without turning all subjects into
one theme. It will be revised during detailed course design.

## 7. Subject progressions

### 7.1 Language arts, literature, and communication

| Band | Core progression |
| --- | --- |
| K–2 | Oral language and read-aloud knowledge; systematic phonemic awareness and phonics; handwriting, spelling, sentence construction; decodable reading paired with rich authentic text; retelling, questioning, and joyful storytelling |
| 3–5 | Morphology, vocabulary, fluency, and knowledge-rich reading; narrative, explanatory, and opinion-to-argument writing; grammar and revision in context; poetry, drama, mythology, biography, history, and science text |
| 6–8 | Disciplinary literacy; close reading without text worship; source comparison; rhetoric foundations; sustained narrative, exposition, argument, research, speaking, and discussion |
| 9–12 | Culturally broad literature and nonfiction; rhetoric and composition; research methods and citation; media criticism; seminars; public speaking; technical and creative writing |
| 13–14 | Advanced composition, rhetoric, logic, interpretation, research synthesis, peer review, publication, and oral defense |

**Design rule:** foundational reading is systematic, cumulative, and highly
practiced. Comprehension is never delayed until decoding is perfect; rich oral
language and knowledge grow alongside the code.

### 7.2 Mathematics

Starbound mathematics combines the Singapore emphasis on mathematical problem
solving and relational understanding with Davydov-inspired attention to
quantity, comparison, equality, structure, and generalization.

| Band | Core progression |
| --- | --- |
| K–2 | Compare and measure quantities; part-whole and difference relationships; equality; number construction; place value; operations as transformations; shape, space, measure, patterns, early data; fluent facts built from relationships |
| 3–5 | Multiplicative reasoning; fractions as numbers and relationships; decimals; ratio seeds; area and volume; coordinate ideas; data; generalization and multiple representations |
| 6–8 | Ratios, proportionality, rational numbers, expressions, equations, functions, geometry, probability, statistics, proof habits, and computational modelling |
| 9–12 | Algebra, geometry, functions, modelling, probability and statistics, discrete structures, and an advanced pathway matched to goals; proof and mathematical communication throughout |
| 13–14 | Quantitative reasoning for citizenship and scholarship; statistical inference; data ethics; optional calculus, linear algebra, discrete math, or domain-specific modelling |

Every year contains concepts, skills, processes, metacognition, and attitudes.
Fluency matters; it is built on meaning and used in non-routine problems rather
than treated as the whole of mathematics.

### 7.3 Science and engineering

| Band | Core progression |
| --- | --- |
| K–2 | Observable patterns; pushes and pulls; light, sound, weather, materials, needs of living things, habitats, sky; careful observation, prediction, simple measurement, fair comparisons, and model revision |
| 3–5 | Matter, energy, waves, forces, Earth systems, space, heredity, ecosystems, adaptation, and engineering design through coherent phenomena and investigations |
| 6–8 | Integrated physical, life, Earth, and space science; controlled investigation; systems and models; scale, energy and matter, cause and effect, stability and change; data and uncertainty |
| 9–12 | Laboratory biology, chemistry, physics, and Earth/space or environmental science; mathematical modelling; engineering; scientific literature and ethics |
| 13–14 | Interdisciplinary science, replication, statistics, field/lab methods, philosophy and history of science, and a research or design investigation |

NGSS three-dimensional learning provides the practice structure: disciplinary
core ideas are used with science and engineering practices and crosscutting
concepts to explain phenomena or design solutions.

### 7.4 History, geography, civics, and economics

| Band | Core progression |
| --- | --- |
| K–2 | Self, family, community, rules, needs and wants, maps, timelines, symbols, work, stories of people and places, and age-appropriate multiple perspectives |
| 3–5 | Broad world geography and civilizational foundations; Indigenous histories; exploration and exchange; early U.S. history; government and economics; source habits |
| 6–8 | Connected world and U.S. history from ancient societies through the modern era; geography, civics, and economics integrated with primary and secondary sources |
| 9–12 | Rigorous world and U.S. history; government, law, economics, psychology/sociology options; historiography, statistics, media, deliberation, and informed civic action |
| 13–14 | Political thought, comparative institutions, global history, economics, ethics, law, and a source-based civic or historical inquiry |

The C3 Inquiry Arc supplies a recurring method: develop questions, use
disciplinary concepts, evaluate sources and evidence, communicate conclusions,
and take informed action. Inquiry is built on real content knowledge.

The exact 60-credit course distribution, general-education core, and intended

arts” means disciplined freedom of mind, not a single cultural canon presented
as the whole human story.

## 9. Time, rhythm, and interdisciplinary work

### Daily anchors

- reading and oral language;
- writing or composition;
- mathematics;
- movement and outdoor time; and
- learner reflection or planning.

### Weekly guarantees

- hands-on science or engineering;
- history/geography/civics;
- music and visual/dramatic art;
- Spanish communication;
- computing/making;
- practical life; and
- unstructured play and self-directed creation.

### Project rule

At least one substantial interdisciplinary project occurs each year. It must
strengthen named subject knowledge rather than dissolve subjects into a vague
theme. A project about rivers can integrate ecology, measurement, mapping,
history, writing, art, and policy while still teaching and assessing each
discipline accurately.

Screen time is chosen by instructional need. A simulation may reveal an
invisible system; a real magnet, garden, book, conversation, instrument, walk,
or measuring cup is often better.

## 10. Assessment and evidence

Starbound separates four ideas that schools often blur:

1. **Exposure:** the learner encountered an idea.
2. **Supported performance:** the learner succeeded with recorded help.
3. **Independent mastery:** the learner met a defined criterion without
   disqualifying support.
4. **Durable transfer:** the learner retrieved and used the knowledge later in
   a different context.

### Evidence system

- embedded formative checks during learning;
- brief retrieval after spacing;
- work samples with dates and revision history;
- audio/video explanations when useful and consented to;
- reading, lab, composition, code, and project logs;
- difficulty and supports used;
- standards and competency crosswalks;
- exhibitions and oral defenses;
- parent/mentor observations; and
- periodic external measures used as calibration, not the curriculum's master.

The portfolio should show growth, best work, false starts, revision, and
independent transfer. Points and avatar levels motivate play but never replace
the academic evidence record.

### Open challenge and testing out

Learners may attempt any published capstone, boss challenge, placement task, or
advanced performance available to them, regardless of the recommended sequence.
The curriculum may advise; it may not hide advanced learning behind seat time.

When a learner succeeds:

- award credit for each outcome directly and validly demonstrated;
- permit tested-out lessons or units to become optional where prerequisite
  evidence is complete;
- record the task version, conditions, supports, score or rubric evidence, and
  date;
- distinguish a narrow advanced talent from mastery of every skill normally
  taught before it;
- use a brief confirmation task when prerequisite evidence is incomplete;
- schedule delayed retrieval before describing the result as durable transfer;
  and
- allow the learner to continue advancing or return to the recommended path.

An unsuccessful attempt carries no academic or game penalty. It creates a
diagnostic map and a suggested training route. The right to attempt the boss is
also a right to discover that more preparation would be useful.

## 11. Difficulty and adaptive development

Normal, Hard, and Master alter scaffolding, representation, complexity, and
transfer demand. They do not rank children.

The long-term adaptive system should behave like a transparent game director:

- estimate a baseline separately for each skill;
- recommend, never secretly impose, a difficulty change;
- explain the evidence behind the recommendation;
- increase challenge by fading support and deepening transfer, not simply by
  adding length or speed;
- detect fragile mastery through later retrieval;
- protect the option to return to Normal immediately;
- keep advanced boss challenges visible and attemptable regardless of the
  recommended baseline;
- preserve parent control and learner dignity; and
- keep sensitive child data local or family-controlled wherever practical.

The build order is manual choice first, interpretable rules second, knowledge
tracing third, and a small adaptive model only after there is enough valid data
and an audit process. An opaque model should never decide what a child is
capable of becoming.

## 12. K–2: the first detailed build

The architecture now narrows. K–2 receives complete sequences before later
bands receive full courses.

The detailed implementation is maintained in the
[K–2 Curriculum and Pacing Guide](K2_CURRICULUM_AND_PACING_GUIDE.md).

### Two learner lanes, one world

**Robby lane — Kindergarten to Grade 1**

- systematic phonemic awareness and phonics with one feature held stable long
  enough for real practice;
- small steps, many meaningful repetitions, and strong oral language;
- concrete quantity and relationship models;
- short read-aloud-supported missions;
- the same Spanish, music, science play, humor, characters, and world status as
  Tommy; and
- optional challenge without skipping foundations.

**Tommy lane — Grade 2 with Grades 3–5 stretch**

- advanced, fast-moving original stories with controlled skill targets where
  needed;
- explicit vocabulary, morphology, comprehension, inference, and writing;
- fewer decoding supports where evidence warrants;
- deeper mathematical explanation and unfamiliar transfer;
- longer investigations and source work; and
- Windows Upward that expose later ideas without converting exposure to
  mastery.

### First complete K–2 unit in each domain

| Domain | Robby core | Tommy core | Shared creation/transfer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundational reading | Short `a`: hear, segment, map, blend, read, spell, and discriminate across a multi-lesson sequence | Word transformations, vowel patterns, morphology seeds, and a rapid story mystery | Build a creature-name and transformation card set |
| Literature/comprehension | Rich read-aloud, retell, character wants, sequence | High-interest original episode, inference, evidence, foreshadowing window | Record or stage an alternate scene |
| Writing | Oral rehearsal, handwriting pattern, complete sentence, label and caption | Sentence combining, paragraph shape, revision for pace and humor | Publish a field-guide page |
| Mathematics | Compare lengths/quantities and express same/different/more/less before symbol fluency | Additive comparison and equality across models, symbols, and story problems | Design a fair supply system for two ridiculous heroes |
| Science | Pushes, pulls, motion, and prediction with real objects | Force diagrams, fair tests, measurement, and gravity window | Build and revise a rescue-course machine |
| Spanish | `Hola`, `¿Cómo estás?`, `yo/tú`, family, identity, wants, likes, food, and needs in small spirals | Same communicative sequence with expanded responses and reading | Make and perform a tiny café/family scene using real-speaker clips |
| Music | Beat, quarter/rest, pitch direction, color-change feedback, and draggable notation | Same lab with layered rhythm, melodic pattern, and form challenges | Save, title, perform, and star an original loop |
| History/geography | Family/community, then-and-now, map symbols | Local sources, timelines, viewpoint, community systems | Build an interactive map and oral history page |
| Computing/making | Sequence and debug a character path | Conditions, events, and simple data | Program a scene from the shared story |
| Health/practical life | Feelings, help, safety, movement, snack preparation | Planning, teamwork, nutrition evidence, simple budgeting | Host the First Star Festival |

### Required structure for every K–2 unit

- 3–5 learn-and-practice sections per lesson or mission cluster;
- one clearly identified governing relationship;
- enough cumulative practice to stabilize the target;
- a rich optional read-along textbook chapter;
- real creation or experimentation after the required sequence;
- Normal, Hard, and Master variants designed from the same concept;
- at least one delayed retrieval;
- one optional Window Upward;
- exact standards and source metadata in the parent layer; and
- dated portfolio evidence with support level recorded.

## 13. Build and validation order

### Phase 1 — Architecture and proof of learning

1. Freeze the common lesson, mastery, evidence, and accessibility schemas.
2. Fully specify the K–2 reading/phonics vertical slice.
3. Test it with Robby and Tommy; observe confusion, voluntary play, transfer,
   and fatigue rather than relying only on completion.
4. Revise the shared lesson engine.

### Phase 2 — One complete K–2 unit per subject

Build mathematics, science, Spanish, music, writing, history/geography, and
computing using the corrected engine. Each receives a student experience,
optional textbook, parent guide, standards crosswalk, and portfolio evidence.

### Phase 3 — K–2 coherent year map

Create full scope-and-sequence documents, prerequisite maps, spaced retrieval
calendar, reading/text map, assessment blueprint, and Florida reporting
crosswalk. Commission expert review in foundational literacy, mathematics,
science, exceptional student education/accessibility, and Florida compliance.

### Phase 4 — Expand by tested band

Develop Grades 3–5 only after K–2 produces reliable learning evidence and a
stable authoring system. Later bands inherit proven architecture but receive
new developmental and subject-matter review.

## 16. Decisions to revisit with evidence

- the exact historical and literary sequence in each year;
- the mathematics course organization after Grade 8;
- the balance between integrated and separate sciences in middle school;
- expected Spanish proficiency by Grades 5, 8, 12, and 14;
- credentialing, authorization, accreditation, or dual-enrollment partnerships
  for the Associate of Arts stage;
- the external measures used for calibration;
- accessibility accommodations and alternative demonstrations of mastery;
- the threshold for any adaptive model to influence recommendations; and
- the legal and evidence requirements for eventual Florida instructional
  material adoption, accreditation, scholarship use, or other certification
  pathway.

The architecture is intentionally ambitious. Its credibility will come from
the quality of the detailed sequences, the transparency of the evidence, and
what the children can independently understand, create, remember, and use.
