## World, Experience, and Pedagogy Design Bible

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** November 11, 2026

## 1. The promise

Starbound Academy is a game world in which reading, writing, mathematics,
science, music, languages, and the liberal arts are not quizzes placed between
the fun. They are the powers, tools, relationships, and laws that make the fun
possible.

Children should experience:

- an anime world they want to remember;
- the experimental intrigue of a great science show;
- the immediate clarity and layered depth of a Nintendo game;
- the conceptual power of Soviet developmental education;
- the agency, play, reflection, and inclusion associated with Norway's
  educational ideals; and
- a standards-aligned education whose evidence is quietly preserved for the
  parent.

The central success question is not merely, "Did the child complete the
lesson?" It is:

> After the required learning was complete, did the child voluntarily keep
> experimenting?

An hour spent voluntarily moving music notes, hearing the results, and making
new compositions is the quality bar for every subject.

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

1. **The academic concept is the mechanic.** A correct answer must not merely
   purchase access to an unrelated game.
2. **Play is a learning requirement.** Humor, surprise, experimentation,
   creation, and curiosity are part of instructional quality.
3. **Concepts come before empty procedures.** Children encounter important
   relationships, model them, describe them, and only then automate procedures.
4. **The child acts before the system explains everything.** Prediction and
   purposeful action create a reason to learn.
5. **Every lesson contains supported success and meaningful stretch.** Normal,
   Hard, and Master change support and depth without changing a child's worth.
6. **Advanced ideas appear early as invitations.** They are never used to shame
   a child or create hidden prerequisite gaps.
7. **Assessment is embedded in performance.** The world observes what the child
   can do; the child does not spend the day staring at test screens.
8. **The child sees an adventure; the parent sees the education.** Standards,
   dates, assistance, evidence, and next steps remain available in the adult
   layer.
9. **Creation matters as much as consumption.** Children compose, build, write,
   test, explain, and save work.
10. **Privacy is part of the product.** A child's learning history is not an
    advertising profile.
11. **The Boss Door is always open.** A learner may attempt the latest challenge
    at any time and receives accurate credit for whatever the performance
    validly proves.

## 3. The four-part creative formula

### Anime attachment

Borrow the storytelling craft, not protected characters or worlds:

- recurring heroes, rivals, mentors, creatures, and comic antagonists;
- rule-governed powers that become more interesting as they are understood;
- training arcs and visible growth;
- mysteries, lore, transformations, and dramatic reversals;
- food, sleeping, fighting, friendship, embarrassment, and absurdly intense
  reactions to small events;
- long-running consequences that reward memory of earlier adventures; and
- serious problems interrupted by ordinary, ridiculous needs.

### Science-show intrigue

- Open with a phenomenon, contradiction, accident, or impossible claim.
- Ask for a prediction before giving the explanation.
- Let the child change one variable and see what happens.
- Repeat the governing idea through several different representations.
- Connect the lesson to something enormous, tiny, gross, funny, or unexpected.
- End by opening a larger question.

### Nintendo intuitiveness

- The first meaningful action is obvious.
- The first attempt is safe.
- The environment teaches the rule before an instruction panel does.
- One new mechanic appears at a time.
- Input and feedback remain consistent across the world.
- Failure is informative, reversible, and often funny.
- A beginner can finish; an expert can discover depth.
- The interface stays quiet until information is needed.

### Soviet developmental learning

- **Elkonin:** analyze sounds and word structure with external objects before
  expecting automatic internal performance.
- **Galperin:** move from material action, to visible model, to spoken
  explanation, to reduced support, to internal independent action.
- **Davydov:** begin with general relationships among quantities and structures,
  then derive numbers, symbols, procedures, and particular cases.
- **Vygotskian guidance:** work just beyond present independence with help that
  is gradually removed.
- **Zankov-style productive difficulty:** present intellectually serious ideas
  early, provided that challenge remains supported and developmentally useful.

The universal Starbound learning movement is:

> Act it → model it → explain it → internalize it → transfer it.

### Why these traditions belong together

The four influences solve different problems. None is sufficient alone.

| Influence | Design problem it solves | Failure if used badly | Starbound control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anime storytelling | Children remember characters, rules, transformations, rivalries, and long arcs voluntarily | Empty spectacle, imitation, endless exposition, or combat unrelated to learning | Original world; the academic relationship governs powers and plot consequences |
| Science-show curiosity | A surprising phenomenon creates a real reason to seek an explanation | Entertainment that reveals the answer before the child predicts or investigates | Prediction, variable change, evidence, model, and a larger unanswered question |
| Nintendo-style game design | Players learn through safe action, consistent feedback, progressive disclosure, and layered challenge | Polished clicking, manipulative reward loops, or difficulty based only on speed | First action teaches; failure informs; expert depth comes from the same mechanic |
| Soviet developmental pedagogy | Material and social action become explicit models, language, abstraction, independence, and transfer | Rigid scripts, unsupported difficulty, or authority replacing thought | Visible orienting model, guided action, explanation, fading support, independent transfer |
| Nordic agency and play | Learners need ownership, participation, wellbeing, creation, and room to explore | Unstructured discovery that leaves novices without powerful knowledge | Explicit teaching supplies tools; learner choice expands as competence grows |

The synthesis is therefore not “education hidden under a game skin.” It is a
single causal chain:

> Attachment earns attention → curiosity creates a need to know → intuitive
> action makes the idea manipulable → developmental instruction makes the
> relationship conscious → practice stabilizes it → agency turns it into
> voluntary exploration and creation.

Each link must be observable in the lesson design. If a story beat, animation,
point award, or interaction cannot explain which link it strengthens, it is a
candidate for removal.

### The play-and-rigor compact

Accreditation-level rigor and play are joint constraints:

- Rigor means important outcomes, coherent prerequisites, accurate content,
  qualified review, valid assessment, retained evidence, and demonstrated
  improvement. It does not mean humorlessness or unnecessary workload.
- Play means agency inside a rule-governed system, experimentation, feedback,
  imagination, creation, and voluntary continuation. It does not mean an
  unrelated minigame placed after a worksheet.
- Narrative may raise the emotional stakes but may never change the academic
  truth.
- Rewards may recognize effort and demonstrated achievement but may never
  substitute for evidence.
- A beginner receives more orientation and support, not a less truthful model.
- An expert receives deeper transfer, variation, and creation, not merely more
  repetitions.

The product succeeds only when an external academic reviewer can inspect the
learning and a child still asks to keep playing.

## 4. The two interfaces

### What the child sees

- missions, powers, experiments, mysteries, rivals, collections, and creations;
- an evolving avatar inside a persistent world;
- immediate reactions to meaningful actions;
- Normal, Hard, and Master choices;
- optional hidden rooms containing advanced ideas;
- personal progress, cosmetics, companions, and saved creations; and
- funny characters whose mistakes invite the child to think.

### What the parent sees

- exact learner, subject, skill, and standards;
- date attempted and date independently demonstrated;
- difficulty selected and difficulty demonstrated;
- supports, hints, read-aloud, and retries used;
- common error patterns and corrective experiences;
- saved writing, compositions, models, explanations, and project photographs;
- scheduled retrieval and recommended next steps;
- reading-material and source log;
- license and attribution information; and
- an exportable, printable best-work portfolio.

Standards codes do not need to interrupt a six-year-old's story. They must still
exist, accurately and inspectably, in the adult layer.

## 5. The Starbound episode loop

A full lesson behaves like a short anime episode and a well-designed game level.

1. **Cold open — Remember**  
   Retrieve one or two ideas from an earlier mission through action.

2. **Something ridiculous goes wrong — Orient**  
   A character encounters a problem whose solution requires today's concept.

3. **The first experiment — Act**  
   The child predicts, touches, moves, combines, sorts, or tests.

4. **The governing relationship — Model**  
   The world makes the important structure visible.

5. **Training montage — Practice**  
   Several short repetitions vary the context while preserving the concept.

6. **The complication — Explain**  
   The easy rule meets an exception, a new representation, or a rival's claim.

7. **Boss problem — Show what you know**  
   The child performs independently enough to produce portfolio evidence.

8. **Creative free play — Transfer**  
   Compose, build, write, experiment, or solve an optional unfamiliar case.

9. **Comedy ending and after-credits reveal — Wonder**  
   Resolve the episode with character humor and open a Window Upward.

The sequence may be divided across short sessions for younger learners. A
lesson is not required to be long in order to be complete.

## 6. Windows Upward

Advanced ideas should feel random to the child and be deliberate to the
curriculum designer.

A Window Upward:

- connects to the current lesson's governing relationship;
- takes roughly two to five minutes;
- is optional and carries no mastery penalty;
- invites manipulation, prediction, or wonder rather than memorization;
- may award a small curiosity bonus;
- is recorded as exposure, not mastery; and
- returns later in a more formal form.

Examples:

| Current concept | Window Upward |
| --- | --- |
| Short-a phonics | Compare sound waves for `/ă/` and `/ō/`. |
| CVC word changes | Discover that one phoneme can transform meaning. |
| Plot sequence | Glimpse foreshadowing or an unreliable witness. |
| Comparing lengths | Express the relationship as `A = B + C`. |
| Addition | Discover subtraction as the inverse transformation. |
| Gravity | Explore orbit as continuous falling. |
| Magnets | Reveal Earth's magnetic field and solar storms. |
| Quarter notes | Experience two half-beats as one beat, then a polyrhythm. |
| Spanish pronouns | Notice why Spanish can omit `yo`. |
| Sentence length | Change narrative tension by changing rhythm and syntax. |

The pattern is:

> Notice now → play later → name eventually → understand deeply.

## 7. Difficulty: Normal, Hard, and Master

Difficulty describes the relationship between challenge and support. It does
not label the learner.

| Mode | Support | Cognitive demand | Base reward |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Normal | Concrete models, visible cues, audio help, guided language | Grade-level target | 10 points |
| Hard | Fewer cues, more construction, explanation required | Deeper application | 15 points |
| Master | Minimal support, unfamiliar transfer, multiple relationships | Grade 3–5 stretch where appropriate | 20 points |

Rules:

- The child may switch modes at any time.
- Returning to Normal never erases progress.
- Practice errors never remove points.
- Hard and Master must not introduce untaught prerequisites and pretend they are
  merely harder versions of the same skill.
- Reading speed does not earn bonus points.
- Master is not simply a longer passage or a faster enemy.
- A child earns the difficulty reward by demonstrating the task, not by clicking
  the difficulty label.
- Replaying can improve the mastery record but cannot generate unlimited points.
- Parents may lock, recommend, or freely allow difficulty switching.
- The system may suggest a mode and must explain why.

The best difficulty adjustment often removes a support rather than adding more
work.

### The Boss Door is always open

Starbound progression is a map, not a locked hallway. Like walking toward the
final danger with three hearts and a wooden sword, a learner may attempt the
newest or most advanced available challenge at any time.

Rules:

- The world may recommend preparation but must not require the child to grind
  through already-mastered work merely to unlock a challenge.
- Major boss challenges are visible from early in the journey, with honest
  information about the knowledge, tools, and likely difficulty involved.
- The attempt must be safe: no lost mastery, removed points, public shame,
  broken streak, or permanent penalty for failure.
- Failure should reveal something useful—a mechanic, prerequisite, strategy,
  question, or future possibility—and offer a direct route to training.
- A child may retreat, prepare, and return without the game treating that
  choice as defeat.
- If the child independently passes a valid advanced assessment, Starbound
  awards mastery credit for every outcome the performance actually
  demonstrates.
- A pass may test out of earlier missions when the boss supplies sufficient
  evidence of those prerequisites.
- The system must not infer mastery of an unobserved skill merely because that
  skill commonly precedes the challenge.
- A short confirmation encounter may be used when the boss provides strong but
  incomplete evidence for a prerequisite.
- Exceptional performance unlocks appropriate rewards, story recognition, and
  harder territory without permanently forcing the new baseline.

The child-facing message is:

> You may knock on any door. The world will tell you the truth about what
> happened, give you credit for what you proved, and keep the path back open.

This rule protects giftedness, uneven development, curiosity, and courage. It
also prevents sequencing from becoming bureaucracy. A seven-year-old who can
solve a Grade 5 problem deserves to try it; a learner who succeeds deserves an
accurate record of that success.

## 8. Points, levels, and the avatar

### Three distinct progress systems

Starbound must not confuse time spent, character status, and academic mastery.

1. **Adventure Points** drive the avatar's long-running level.
2. **Mastery Sparks** represent demonstrated academic skills.
3. **Artifacts** preserve creations and best work.

A child may have a high character level because of years of committed learning
without being described as academically superior to someone else. Subject
mastery remains skill-specific.

### Point sources

| Action | Suggested points |
| --- | ---: |
| Complete Normal independently | 10 |
| Complete Hard independently | 15 |
| Complete Master independently | 20 |
| Scheduled retrieval mission | 3–5 |
| Window Upward exploration | 1–2 |
| Save a meaningful creation | 2, with a sensible daily cap |
| Improve an earlier explanation | Difference or small growth bonus |
| Help explain a concept aloud | Reflection bonus, not automatic mastery |

No points for advertisements, streak anxiety, infinite clicking, or staying
online longer than is educationally useful.

### Character level

- Character level spans subjects and years.
- Early levels arrive quickly; later levels require progressively more points.
- A transparent curve can begin with `100 + 25 × (current level - 1)` points to
  the next level and be tuned after observing real use.
- Level never resets at the end of a grade.
- School-year and subject records remain separate from lifetime character level.
- Robby and Tommy do not share a competitive leaderboard.

### Avatar growth

The avatar should evolve for years without becoming embarrassingly childish:

- clothing, colors, hair, equipment, rooms, ships, instruments, pets, poses,
  trails, and emotes;
- earned cosmetics connected to meaningful achievements;
- subject tools that visually develop without implying permanent ability labels;
- a home base displaying saved music, writing, discoveries, and trophies;
- optional companion characters with humorous behavior; and
- age-resilient art direction that can mature from playful animation to graphic
  novel, science-fiction, and mythic styles.

Cosmetics should express identity, not pressure purchases. The private family
version contains no monetized loot boxes or artificial scarcity.

The full avatar, wardrobe, personal gallery, future community, and mod-library
architecture is specified in the
[Avatar, Community, and Mod Ecosystem](AVATAR_COMMUNITY_AND_MOD_ECOSYSTEM.md).

### Future community posture

Starbound may eventually become a safe asynchronous commons where learners
exhibit stories, music, experiments, designs, explanations, and lessons. It
must never become a child-focused social network:

- no advertising or sale of attention;
- no infinite feed, followers, public popularity score, or engagement bait;
- no open private messaging between children;
- finite purpose-built galleries, workshops, and exhibitions;
- parent-controlled audiences and sharing;
- useful structured feedback rather than reaction chasing; and
- private participation remains a complete educational path.

Community-created lessons and worlds may enter a reviewed mod library. They do
not become official curriculum or generate official credit until they pass the
same academic, accessibility, source, safety, and governance gates as Starbound
material.

## 9. The world

### Four age worlds, one continuous journey

Starbound Academy has four major experience worlds. These are not reskins of
one map. Each is a new era of the learner's journey with its own geography,
presentation density, humor, social structure, game forms, music, and visual
language.

| Experience world | Academic home | Form and feeling | Character of play |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Wonder Archipelago** | Kindergarten–Grade 5 | Luminous floating islands, creature habitats, moons, forests, workshops, plazas, and a warm moving home base | Immediate cause and effect, tactile manipulation, exploration, stories, collection, experiments, creation, and spectacular readable feedback |
| **The Guild Realms** | Grades 6–8 | Interconnected guild cities, archives, laboratories, wild expedition zones, observatories, studios, and contested systems | Apprenticeship, tools of the disciplines, investigations, strategy, building, source mysteries, team roles, and defended explanations |
| **The Constellation Network** | Grades 9–12 | A mature network of campuses, research stations, civic districts, historical portals, industries, ecosystems, and deep-space problems | Courses, simulations, laboratories, seminars, internships, consequential choices, specialization, and capstone-scale projects |
| **The Great Conversation** | Associate of Arts stage | A collegiate world of libraries, forums, laboratories, ateliers, archives, cultural crossroads, and an evolving thesis observatory | Seminar, close reading, proof, research, fieldwork, studio practice, public argument, synthesis, and original contribution |

The Wonder Archipelago contains two instructional regions: the **Launch
Region** for K–2 and the expanding **Frontier Region** for Grades 3–5. They
share a coherent world and cast, but the Frontier opens larger islands, longer
quests, denser systems, and greater independence. This preserves developmental
precision without forcing a visual reset halfway through elementary school.

The transition between age worlds should feel like reaching a new continent in
a long-running adventure. It never erases the avatar, level, wardrobe, home,
creations, companions, portfolio, accomplishments, or learning history. Earlier
worlds remain visitable. Future-world challenge portals and major bosses can be
visible and attempted early; the interface may explain likely prerequisites,
but character level does not lock the door. Formal course credit still follows
the evidence and authorization rules of that stage.

Across all four worlds, navigation grammar remains learnable: Home, Worlds,
Create, and Me retain their meaning. Their visual form and information density
mature. K–5 uses large landmarks and narrated invitations; 6–8 adds maps,
toolbelts, guild boards, and project logs; 9–12 adds course dashboards, research
notebooks, calendars, and data views; the A.A. stage adds syllabi, seminars,
credit maps, citations, and degree-audit views without losing the world itself.

### The Wonder Archipelago: K–5 subject islands

The current subject worlds become the first islands and districts of the Wonder
Archipelago:

| Island or district | Subject | Governing fantasy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story Nebula | Reading | Sounds, words, evidence, and interpretation reveal paths. |
| Writer's Forge | Writing | Sentences and stories physically reshape scenes. |
| Measure Moon | Mathematics | Relationships stabilize machines and structures. |
| Force Frontier | Science | Prediction and experiment control natural systems. |
| Rhythm Grove | Music | Pitch, rhythm, form, and composition animate the world. |
| Plaza del Sol | Spanish | Communication creates relationships and solves social needs. |

Later islands and districts may include:

- Chronicle Archipelago — history and primary sources;
- Civic Citadel — government, economics, law, and civic reasoning;
- Code Foundry — computing and systems thinking;
- Logic Labyrinth — logic, rhetoric, probability, and philosophy;
- Living Atlas — geography, ecology, and cultures;
- Art Atelier — visual art, design, architecture, and media; and
- portals that foreshadow the Guild Realms, Constellation Network, and Great
  Conversation.

Each island has its own mechanics, but the avatar, characters, lore, portfolio,
and learning record connect them. Later age worlds reinterpret disciplines
through more mature structures rather than merely enlarging the same island.

## 10. Cast and humor bible

The cast must be original and persistent. Initial roles:

- **The heroic improviser:** talented, generous, and prone to acting before
  understanding the problem.
- **The literal rival:** brilliant, competitive, and defeated by metaphor,
  sarcasm, and social ambiguity.
- **The hungry creature:** turns academic problems into food emergencies.
- **The sleeping scientist:** misses ordinary events and wakes instantly for
  genuinely astonishing phenomena.
- **The punctuation robot:** treats commas, dialogue marks, and ambiguous
  sentences as public-safety emergencies.
- **The visiting alien:** learns English and Spanish while navigating awkward
  human customs.
- **The tiny antagonist:** makes excellent plans that fail because of naps,
  snacks, friendship, or one misunderstood variable.

### Humor rules

- Joke through situation, timing, character, language, and system reaction.
- Let characters be wrong so the child can correct them safely.
- Never make the child's error the target of ridicule.
- Wrong answers should produce useful consequences, not only a red X.
- Use dramatic overreaction sparingly enough that it remains funny.
- Permit absurd examples when the underlying academic representation stays
  accurate.
- Food, sleep, harmless combat, awkward conversation, mistaken confidence, and
  literal interpretation are recurring comic engines.
- Do not imitate dialogue, visual design, signature moves, or lore from existing
  anime or game properties.

Example feedback:

> The magnet inspected the banana and said, "Absolutely not." What property
> should we test next?

## 11. Subject design

### Reading and phonics

**Mechanics:** sound chambers, phoneme pods, word transformations, read-along
tracking, evidence collection, plot paths, point-of-view lenses, and story
reconstruction.

**Pedagogy:** explicit systematic phonics for beginning readers; daily connected
text; rich oral language and read-aloud knowledge beyond the child's decoding
level; repeated reading; discussion; text evidence; and original high-velocity
fiction.

**Anchors:** Florida B.E.S.T.; New York and Massachusetts ELA; UFLI Foundations;
Core Knowledge Language Arts as a noncommercial reference or optional linked
resource; and original Starbound readers.

### Writing

**Mechanics:** movable sentence parts, dialogue control, verb-driven animation,
punctuation reactions, scene construction, revision comparisons, and branching
story consequences.

**Pedagogy:** manipulate → rehearse aloud → write → revise → publish; narrative,
informative, opinion/argument, and explanation; sentence combining; spelling and
encoding connected to phonics.

**Anchor book:** *Writer's Forge: Build, Transform, and Create*.

### Mathematics

**Mechanics:** compare quantities, balance systems, construct units, transform
models, repair relational machines, discover invariants, and generalize rules.

**Pedagogy:** Davydov-inspired quantity, comparison, measurement, equality,
general relationships, modeling, symbolic expression, arithmetic, fluency, and
transfer.

**Anchors:** Florida B.E.S.T.; first-edition Illustrative Mathematics K–5 where
license and fit permit; original Starbound developmental sequence.

**Anchor book:** *Relations Before Rules: Starbound Mathematics*.

### Science

**Mechanics:** prediction, controlled variables, simulated and household
experiments, evidence capture, modeling invisible systems, and explanation.

**Pedagogy:** phenomenon → question → prediction → investigation → model →
explanation → new case.

**Anchors:** current Florida science standards, NGSS practices and concepts,
OpenSciEd as a noncommercial reference or optional linked resource, Smithsonian,
NASA, NOAA, museums, and original investigations.

**Anchor book:** *The Young Scientist's Field Manual*.

### Music

**Mechanics:** move notation, hear immediate results, color-code pitch, loop,
vary tempo and duration, perform, improvise, compose, save, and replay.

**Pedagogy:** hear → reproduce → represent → transform → compose → explain.

**Anchors:** Florida arts standards, National Core Arts Standards, Carnegie Hall
Music Educators Toolbox, Smithsonian Folkways, public-domain music, and original
audio/notation activities.

**Anchor book:** *The Color Music Lab*.

### Spanish

**Mechanics:** greet, feed, help, direct, identify, ask, answer, assemble phrases,
and resolve comic social situations using language.

**Pedagogy:** one communicative function at a time; authentic listening;
rehearsed language followed by recombination and spontaneous use; cumulative
high-frequency vocabulary; culture integrated with language.

**Anchors:** ACTFL proficiency guidance and Can-Do progression; GPB SALSA as an
optional official link; licensed human audio; original Starbound dialogue.

**Anchor book:** *Mi Mundo: Spanish for Everyday Adventures*.

## 12. Standards architecture

Standards are versioned data, not prose scattered throughout game code.

Every skill record should contain:

- stable internal skill ID;
- subject and domain;
- prerequisite skills;
- grade-band floor and optional stretch band;
- Florida benchmark alignment;
- comparison alignments where useful;
- learning objective in observable language;
- accepted evidence types;
- misconception and error tags;
- support options;
- difficulty transformations;
- retrieval schedule; and
- source and review history.

### Current K–2 standards stack

| Subject | Compliance floor | Quality comparison | Instructional reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading/writing | Florida B.E.S.T. ELA | New York, Massachusetts | UFLI, CKLA, IES foundational-reading guidance |
| Mathematics | Florida B.E.S.T. Mathematics | Massachusetts and international progressions | Davydov research, first-edition IM K–5 |
| Science | Current Florida standards | NGSS three-dimensional learning | OpenSciEd, Smithsonian, federal science agencies |
| Music | Florida fine arts | National Core Arts Standards | Carnegie Hall, Smithsonian Folkways |
| Spanish | Florida world languages where applicable | ACTFL | ACTFL Novice progression, authentic media |

Standards versions and effective dates must be recorded because state standards
change.

### K–12 and Associate of Arts horizon

The long vision is a progression and an accreditation-intent design target,
not a claim of present accreditation.

- **K–2: Launch Years** — foundations, oral language, play, concrete action,
  curiosity, and early independence.
- **Grades 3–5: Frontier Years** — knowledge building, multiplication and
  fractions, investigation, sustained reading, projects, and richer worlds.
- **Grades 6–8: Guild Years** — disciplinary tools, formal systems, argument,
  laboratories, history, computing, and collaboration.
- **Grades 9–12: Constellation Years** — rigorous courses, primary sources,
  advanced mathematics and science, rhetoric, research, and capstones.
- **Associate of Arts Stage** — a formal two-year, 60-credit target spanning
  communication, mathematics, natural science, humanities, social science,
  arts, language, transfer-oriented electives, research, and great works across
  cultures. “Grades 13–14” is private timeline shorthand only.

The Associate of Arts stage is designed toward lower-division college rigor.
College credit, an A.A. degree, accreditation, dual enrollment, and transfer
claims require formal authorization, partnerships, and approvals beyond
curriculum quality.

## 13. Adaptive learning roadmap

The useful Fallout analogy is **level-scaled encounters**: the world meets the
player at an appropriate level while preserving exploration. Starbound should
not use opaque rubber-banding that secretly changes rules or fakes success.

### Stage 0 — Learner-controlled difficulty

- Normal, Hard, and Master are always visible.
- The child chooses; the parent can guide.
- The system records outcomes but does not make automated decisions.

### Stage 1 — Transparent rules

Before training any model, use understandable rules based on curriculum logic:

- two independent successes plus later retrieval may trigger a suggestion to
  try Hard;
- repeated errors on the same prerequisite increase concrete support;
- heavy hint dependence prompts a supported example rather than extra volume;
- a single bad session never lowers the long-term estimate;
- success with an advanced challenge records exposure or transfer separately;
- scheduled review is based on elapsed time and prior retrieval, not a daily
  streak; and
- the child may ignore the suggestion.

The parent receives a human-readable reason:

> Suggested Hard because Tommy independently identified cause and evidence in
> two missions and remembered the skill four days later.

### Stage 2 — Interpretable knowledge tracing

After enough high-quality, skill-tagged data exists, introduce an interpretable
student model such as Bayesian Knowledge Tracing or a similarly auditable
probabilistic model.

Estimate mastery separately for each knowledge component. Distinguish:

- prior mastery;
- learning during practice;
- a lucky guess;
- a correct idea expressed with an input mistake;
- a slip despite likely mastery; and
- retention over time.

The model recommends; it does not define the child.

### Stage 3 — Small local model

A later small model may personalize baseline difficulty, support, repetition,
retrieval timing, and task selection. Prefer on-device or family-controlled
inference where practical.

The model may adapt:

- number and spacing of practice opportunities;
- visibility of concrete models;
- type and timing of hints;
- vocabulary support;
- question construction versus recognition;
- complexity of relationships;
- next retrieval date; and
- suggested Normal, Hard, or Master baseline.

The model must not autonomously adapt:

- the truth of instructional content;
- required standards;
- a permanent label of intelligence or potential;
- emotional pressure, streaks, or monetization;
- access to the child's portfolio;
- a grade or diagnosis; or
- unsupervised curriculum generated directly for a child.

Initial adaptive content should be selected from a human-reviewed item and
activity bank. Generative content, if introduced, requires separate validation,
safety, citation, age-appropriateness, and parent-control systems.

### Minimum adaptive safeguards

- Parent can inspect, correct, reset, export, or disable adaptation.
- Child can always choose a different difficulty.
- Every recommendation has a plain-language explanation.
- Mastery estimates include uncertainty.
- No biometric, facial, emotional, advertising, or unrelated behavioral
  profiling.
- Voice need not be recorded or retained to provide read-aloud or pronunciation
  activities.
- Models are evaluated for systematic differences between learners and groups.
- Curriculum experts review whether optimization improves learning rather than
  merely clicks or time-on-platform.

## 14. Assessment and portfolio

### Evidence captured from play

- final response or constructed model;
- level of independence;
- difficulty demonstrated;
- meaningful hints or supports used;
- misconception and correction;
- explanation, writing, composition, design, or experiment;
- date and relevant retrieval interval;
- associated standards and skills;
- optional parent observation; and
- a child-selected best-work flag.

### Portfolio views

- chronological family learning log;
- subject and skill progressions;
- best-work binder;
- music and creation libraries;
- reading-material log;
- standards coverage report;
- narrative growth summary;
- printable year-end portfolio; and
- private JSON backup and restore.

Avoid presenting probabilistic mastery estimates as report-card grades. The
portfolio should show evidence and growth, not convert childhood into a single
score.

## 15. Privacy, safety, and accessibility

### Privacy posture

- Local-first by default.
- No advertising or sale of child data.
- Collect only data required for learning and family records.
- Parent controls identity, export, retention, and deletion.
- Separate child play from parent administration.
- Do not require a child's email address.
- Keep external video and analytics providers out of the core learning record.
- Establish a deletion schedule before any hosted account system exists.
- Design for COPPA from the beginning if the product moves beyond private family
  use; evaluate FERPA obligations before school deployment.

### Accessibility posture

- Full keyboard operation and visible focus.
- Text and controls remain usable without WebGL.
- Reduced-motion mode.
- Captions and transcripts for media.
- Natural English read-aloud and word-level support.
- Color never carries meaning alone.
- Readable typography and adjustable presentation.
- Multiple ways to respond where the construct permits them.
- Do not let TTS answer an independent decoding assessment automatically.
- Preserve challenge while changing access and support.

Three.js and WebGL are progressive enhancement. The semantic lesson, controls,
and evidence remain available through accessible HTML.

## 16. Sources and licensing

Core Starbound lessons, stories, games, graphics, assessments, and textbooks
should be original, public domain, or commercially reusable where feasible.

Noncommercial resources may be used in the private family context when their
terms permit. For future flexibility:

- treat them as optional official links or permitted embeds;
- do not make successful lesson completion depend on them;
- do not download, alter, or rehost without permission;
- retain the source URL, creator, title, license, version, and access date;
- identify whether the use is link, embed, adaptation, quotation, or local asset;
- record whether commercial reuse is allowed;
- provide a tested fallback; and
- isolate the resource behind a content record so it can be replaced.

Nonprofit status does not automatically make a use noncommercial. License
analysis depends on the use, the source's terms, and the relevant law. Obtain
permission or legal review when future distribution falls into a gray area.

## 17. Conceptual content and data model

The implementation should eventually separate these records:

### Learner

Identity chosen by the family, accessibility preferences, avatar state, points,
levels, parent settings, and privacy choices.

### Skill

Stable skill ID, progression, prerequisites, standards, misconceptions,
evidence, supports, and retrieval rules.

### Lesson

Story wrapper, target skills, episode stages, difficulty transforms, assets,
source records, humor beats, Window Upward, and portfolio evidence definition.

### Attempt

Date, difficulty chosen, task version, responses, meaningful supports, outcome,
confidence/uncertainty, and completion status.

### Artifact

Writing, composition, drawing, model, explanation, project photograph, or
parent note with learner ownership and best-work status.

### Source

Provider, URL, license, attribution, commercial status, storage mode, fallback,
and review date.

### Adaptation decision

Recommendation, evidence used, explanation, confidence, parent override, and
result. This audit trail is required before automated adaptation becomes
consequential.

## 18. First reading vertical slices

### Robby — *The Short-A Workshop*

**Floor:** Kindergarten–Grade 1  
**Core:** hear, segment, map, blend, read, and encode short-a CVC words  
**Story:** a hungry alien's word machine keeps producing the wrong food

1. Retrieve known consonant sounds.
2. Hear and identify `/ă/` without print.
3. Move one phoneme pod for every sound.
4. Map the pods to letters.
5. Transform `at → sat → Sam → mat`.
6. Read a controlled, genuinely decodable micro-story.
7. Spell two words from sound.
8. Read a new word and sentence independently.
9. Explore an optional sound-wave Window Upward.

**Normal:** sound chambers, visible letters, immediate model.  
**Hard:** reduced cues, more sound substitution, spelling from audio.  
**Master:** unfamiliar decodable transfer and correction of a misspelling;
never an untaught spelling pattern disguised as difficulty.

### Tommy — *The Storm Core Trial*

**Floor:** Grade 2  
**Stretch:** Grades 3–5 reasoning  
**Core:** plot, cause, character response, inference, and textual evidence  
**Story:** destroying a storm core may save the city or destroy its shield

1. Retrieve plot, character, problem, and evidence.
2. Predict the consequence of the hero's plan.
3. Read a rapid original action story.
4. Reconstruct the event chain in a 3D story arena.
5. Collect evidence cores and attach them to claims.
6. Explain a character's choice.
7. Defend a conclusion independently.
8. Explore a conflicting witness or implied-theme Window Upward.

**Normal:** chunked text, glossary, sequencing, explicit evidence choices.  
**Hard:** reduced scaffolding, motive and cause inference, constructed response.  
**Master:** conflicting viewpoint, implied theme, and two-detail defense.

Both slices schedule brief retrieval after approximately one day, several days,
and one week.

## 19. Quality gates for every lesson

A lesson is not ready merely because it runs.

### Educational

- One narrow core objective is stated and accurately assessed.
- Prerequisites are known.
- The advanced challenge remains attemptable without completing the recommended
  path first.
- Practice is cumulative and sufficient.
- Difficulty changes are valid.
- Independent evidence matches the objective.
- Retrieval is scheduled.
- Standards and sources are reviewed.

### Game design

- The child performs a meaningful action quickly.
- The mechanic embodies the concept.
- Feedback changes the world.
- There is a genuine choice or experiment.
- Failure is safe and useful.
- Free play or creation exists where appropriate.
- Replay changes something meaningful.

### Entertainment

- The episode contains a character desire or conflict.
- Humor emerges from character or concept.
- The story has escalation and payoff.
- A Window Upward creates wonder.
- Copy does not sound like an adult pretending to be a cartoon.

### Product

- Keyboard, mobile, and reduced-motion use work.
- WebGL has a fallback.
- Progress and artifact capture are correct.
- Export/import preserves the record.
- External resources have attribution, fallback, and license records.
- No unnecessary child data leaves the device.

## 20. Measures of success

### Learning

- independent accuracy;
- later retrieval;
- transfer to a new representation or situation;
- quality of explanation;
- reduction in required support; and
- standards coverage supported by actual evidence.

### Engagement

- voluntary continuation after the required mission;
- voluntary replay for experimentation rather than point farming;
- number and variety of meaningful creations;
- use of optional Windows Upward;
- child-initiated questions; and
- willingness to explain discoveries to another person.

### Parent trust

- understandable learning records;
- visible source and standards alignment;
- reliable backups and exports;
- useful next-step recommendations;
- control over adaptive behavior and privacy; and
- a portfolio that resembles a child's intellectual life, not an analytics
  dashboard.

Time-on-platform alone is not success. A shorter session that produces durable
understanding is better than compulsive engagement.

## 21. Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Prove the lesson standard

- Build the Robby and Tommy reading slices.
- Build the original read-along companion chapters.
- Add Normal, Hard, and Master.
- Add Adventure Points, demonstrated difficulty, and avatar foundations.
- Test whether children continue playing voluntarily.

### Phase 2 — Extend the engine

- Apply the episode structure to one exemplary lesson in every current subject.
- Introduce the recurring cast and world lore.
- Add source/license records and standards mapping.
- Strengthen portfolio snapshots and retrieval scheduling.

### Phase 3 — Transparent personalization

- Add rule-based recommendations.
- Add parent explanations and overrides.
- Validate recommendations against observed learning.
- Do not train a model until skill tags and outcomes are trustworthy.

### Phase 4 — Broaden the academy

- Complete K–2 scope and sequence.
- Expand through Grades 3–5 with age-resilient world design.
- Add history, civics, geography, art, and computing.
- Evaluate formal review, accessibility, and adoption requirements.

### Phase 5 — K–12 and Associate of Arts horizon

- Expand one grade band at a time.
- Preserve the avatar and world while maturing tone and visual design.
- Develop high-school courses and capstones.
- Define the Associate of Arts stage with external academic and accreditation
  review.
- Pursue accreditation or credit partnerships only when evidence and governance
  justify the claim.
